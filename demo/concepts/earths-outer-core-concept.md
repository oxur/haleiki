## Quick Definition

Earth's outer core is a fluid layer about 2,260 km thick, composed of mostly iron and nickel, that lies above the solid inner core and below the mantle. Its convective motion generates Earth's magnetic field through the geodynamo mechanism.

## Core Definition

Earth's outer core is a fluid layer about 2,260 km thick, composed of mostly iron and nickel that lies above Earth's solid inner core and below its mantle. It begins approximately 2,889 km beneath Earth's surface at the core-mantle boundary and ends 5,150 km beneath the surface at the inner core boundary ([earths-outer-core](/source/earths-outer-core/)). The outer core is liquid, unlike the solid inner core; evidence comes from seismology, which shows that seismic shear-waves are not transmitted through it ([earths-outer-core](/source/earths-outer-core/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Mantle** — The outer core lies beneath the mantle; understanding Earth's layered structure provides context

## Key Properties

1. Fluid layer approximately 2,260 km thick
2. Composed primarily of iron and nickel with 5-10% light elements
3. Temperature ranges from ~3,000-4,500 K in the outer region to ~4,000-8,000 K near the inner core
4. Low viscosity fluid that convects turbulently
5. Does not transmit seismic shear waves, confirming its liquid state
6. As the core cools, the inner core grows at the expense of the outer core (~1 mm/year)

## Context & Application

The outer core's fluid nature is what makes Earth's magnetic field possible. Without a liquid, electrically conducting layer, there would be no geodynamo, no magnetic field, and consequently no protection from solar wind. The composition of light elements in the outer core constrains models of Earth's accretion and core formation history ([earths-outer-core](/source/earths-outer-core/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([earths-outer-core](/source/earths-outer-core/)): As Earth's core cools, the liquid at the inner core boundary freezes at an estimated rate of 1 mm per year, approximately 80,000 tonnes of iron per second.

## Relationships

- **Earth's inner core** is the solid layer at the center, distinguished by being solid due to extreme pressure
- **Geodynamo** operates within the outer core, generating Earth's magnetic field
- **Mantle** lies above the outer core, separated by the core-mantle boundary

## Common Confusions

- **The outer core is liquid, not the mantle**: People sometimes confuse which layer is fluid; the mantle is predominantly solid but flows slowly, while the outer core is truly liquid
