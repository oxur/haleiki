## Quick Definition

The lithosphere is Earth's rigid outer mechanical layer, comprising the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle. It is broken into tectonic plates whose motion allows heat to escape Earth's interior.

## Core Definition

Earth's lithosphere consists of the crust and the uppermost mantle, forming a single mechanically rigid layer ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)). In the context of plate tectonics, the lithosphere is divided into separate plates that move relative to each other on the underlying, relatively weak asthenosphere ([tectonics](/source/tectonics/)). The structure of Earth can be defined mechanically as lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesospheric mantle, outer core, and inner core ([internal-structure-of-earth](/source/internal-structure-of-earth/)).

## Key Properties

1. Comprises the crust (5-70 km thick) and the uppermost mantle
2. Mechanically rigid, behaving as a solid on human timescales
3. Broken into tectonic plates that move relative to each other
4. Rests on the weaker, more ductile asthenosphere
5. The crust-mantle boundary within the lithosphere is marked by the Mohorovicic discontinuity

## Context & Application

The lithosphere concept is essential for understanding plate tectonics: it is the lithosphere, not just the crust, that constitutes the moving plates. The distinction between lithosphere and asthenosphere is mechanical (based on rigidity), while the distinction between crust and mantle is chemical (based on composition) ([internal-structure-of-earth](/source/internal-structure-of-earth/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)): The lithosphere is broken into tectonic plates whose motion allows heat to escape the interior of Earth into space.

**Example 2** ([tectonics](/source/tectonics/)): In plate tectonics, the lithosphere acts as a single mechanical layer divided into plates moving on the asthenosphere.

## Relationships

- **Plate tectonics** describes the motion of lithospheric plates
- **Asthenosphere** is the weaker layer beneath the lithosphere that allows plate motion
- **Earth's crust** forms the upper portion of the lithosphere
- **Mantle** contributes its uppermost portion to the lithosphere

## Common Confusions

- **Lithosphere vs. crust**: The lithosphere includes both the crust and the rigid uppermost mantle; the crust is only the chemical outer layer
- **Lithosphere vs. tectonic plate**: The terms are related but distinct; the lithosphere is the material, while plates are the discrete units into which it is divided
