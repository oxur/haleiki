## Quick Definition

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the capability of computational systems to perform tasks typically associated with human intelligence -- such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and decision-making -- and the field of research in engineering, mathematics, and computer science that studies methods enabling this capability.

## Core Definition

Artificial intelligence is the capability of computational systems to perform tasks typically associated with human intelligence, such as learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and decision-making. It is a field of research in engineering, mathematics, and computer science that develops and studies methods and software that enable machines to perceive their environment and use learning and intelligence to take actions that maximize their chances of achieving defined goals ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)). AI was founded as an academic discipline in 1956 at the Dartmouth workshop ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/), [dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)). The field went through multiple cycles of optimism followed by "AI winters," with funding and interest increasing substantially after 2012 when deep learning outperformed previous techniques, accelerating further after 2017 with the transformer architecture ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Traditional goals** -- learning, reasoning, knowledge representation, planning, natural language processing, and perception ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).
2. **Key techniques** -- state space search, mathematical optimization, formal logic, artificial neural networks, probabilistic methods, and deep learning ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).
3. **Interdisciplinary** -- draws upon psychology, linguistics, philosophy, neuroscience, economics, and other fields ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).
4. **Multiple paradigms** -- includes symbolic AI (logic, search), statistical/connectionist AI (neural networks, machine learning), and hybrid approaches ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).
5. **Generative AI era** -- since the 2020s, generative AI using large language models and transformer architectures can generate text, images, audio, and video ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).
6. **AGI aspiration** -- some companies aim to create artificial general intelligence (AGI) that can complete virtually any cognitive task as well as a human ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).

## Context & Application

The Dartmouth workshop of 1956 is widely considered the founding event of AI as a field, where John McCarthy coined the term "artificial intelligence" ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)). The field has experienced dramatic growth since 2012, driven by GPU acceleration of neural networks, the availability of large datasets, and architectural innovations like the transformer. Current models and services include ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, and Meta AI ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)). Ethical concerns, AI safety, and potential existential risks have prompted discussions of AI regulation ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([artificial-intelligence](/source/artificial-intelligence/)): Generative pre-trained transformers (GPT) are large language models that generate text based on semantic relationships between words. They are pre-trained on large corpora and then fine-tuned using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

**Example 2** ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)): The 1956 Dartmouth proposal stated: "every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it."
