## Quick Definition

The Edicts of Ashoka are a collection of stone inscriptions from the 3rd century BCE, spread across South Asia, written primarily in Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts (with some in Greek and Aramaic). They represent the earliest significant body of Indic epigraphy.

## Core Definition

The Edicts of Ashoka are a series of 3rd-century BCE inscriptions commissioned by Emperor Ashoka of the Maurya Empire, carved on rocks, pillars, and cave walls across present-day India, Nepal, Pakistan, Afghanistan, and Bangladesh. They are written primarily in the Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts, with some inscriptions in Greek and Aramaic. The edicts contain Ashoka's proclamations on dharma (moral law), religious tolerance, and governance ([edicts-of-ashoka](/source/edicts-of-ashoka/)). The Major Rock Edicts at Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi are written in Kharosthi, and their decipherment was facilitated by the prior reading of Kharosthi from Indo-Greek bilingual coins ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Multi-script** -- written in Brahmi, Kharosthi, Greek, and Aramaic depending on location and audience.
2. **3rd century BCE** -- dating to the reign of Ashoka (c. 268-232 BCE) of the Maurya Empire.
3. **Geographic spread** -- found across South Asia from Afghanistan to Bangladesh.
4. **Multiple formats** -- includes Major Rock Edicts, Minor Rock Edicts, Pillar Edicts, and cave inscriptions.
5. **Dharma content** -- contain Ashoka's moral and administrative proclamations.
6. **Earliest Indic epigraphy** -- among the earliest decipherable inscriptions in Indian scripts.

## Context & Application

The edicts are found at dozens of sites across South Asia, from Kandahar (in Greek and Aramaic) to locations throughout the Indian subcontinent. They represent the first use of Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts in a significant epigraphic corpus. The decipherment of Kharosthi from the edicts was a landmark achievement in South Asian epigraphy ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/), [edicts-of-ashoka](/source/edicts-of-ashoka/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/)): The words "inscription of the dharma" (_dhrāma dipu_) in Edict No. 1 at Shahbazgarhi demonstrate Kharosthi's use in Ashokan inscriptions.

**Example 2** ([edicts-of-ashoka](/source/edicts-of-ashoka/)): The Kandahar inscription is bilingual in Greek and Aramaic, reflecting the Hellenistic cultural influence in the western reaches of Ashoka's empire.
