## Quick Definition

Renaissance music covers European music of the 15th and 16th centuries, characterized by rich polyphonic textures, increasing reliance on thirds and sixths as consonances, and the development of secular forms alongside sacred genres.

## Core Definition

Renaissance music is traditionally understood to cover European music of the 15th and 16th centuries. The period may be roughly subdivided, with an early period corresponding to Guillaume Du Fay, a middle dominated by the Franco-Flemish School and four-part textures, and culminating in the florid counterpoint of Palestrina and the Roman School ([renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)). Music was increasingly freed from medieval constraints, and more variety was permitted in range, rhythm, harmony, form, and notation. On the other hand, rules of counterpoint became more constrained, particularly with regard to treatment of dissonances ([renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)).

## Prerequisites

- **polyphony** -- Rich polyphonic texture is a defining feature of Renaissance music
- **counterpoint** -- Contrapuntal technique became increasingly sophisticated during the Renaissance

## Key Properties

1. Covers European music of the 15th and 16th centuries
2. Defining feature: rich polyphonic texture with four or more independent parts
3. Increasing reliance on thirds and sixths as consonances
4. Modal character, which began breaking down toward functional tonality by the period's end
5. Key genres: mass, motet, madrigal, chanson
6. Printing press (1439) enabled wider distribution of music

## Context & Application

The Renaissance saw music freed from many medieval constraints while paradoxically tightening contrapuntal rules. The invention of the printing press made music widely available for the first time. By the end of the period, the modal system was giving way to functional tonality, setting the stage for the Baroque era ([renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)): The dissemination of chansons, motets, and masses throughout Europe coincided with the unification of polyphonic practice into the fluid style that culminated in Palestrina, Lassus, Tallis, Byrd, and Victoria.
