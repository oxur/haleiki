## Quick Definition

Wave-particle duality is the quantum mechanical principle that every quantum entity exhibits both wave-like and particle-like properties. Measurements of quantum systems show characteristics of both particles and waves, depending on the experimental arrangement.

## Core Definition

Measurements of quantum systems show characteristics of both particles and waves, a phenomenon known as wave-particle duality ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)). The double-slit experiment illustrates this: light passing through two slits produces an interference pattern (wave behavior), yet it is always absorbed at the screen at discrete points as individual particles. Furthermore, detecting which slit a particle passes through destroys the interference pattern, demonstrating that the wave and particle aspects are complementary ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)). In addition to light, electrons, atoms, and molecules all exhibit the same dual behavior.

## Key Properties

1. **Universal** -- wave-particle duality applies to all quantum entities: photons, electrons, atoms, and molecules ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).
2. **Context-dependent** -- the aspect observed (wave or particle) depends on the experimental arrangement and what is measured.
3. **Complementarity** -- measuring particle-like properties (which slit) precludes observing wave-like properties (interference), and vice versa.
4. **Historical development** -- Louis de Broglie proposed matter waves in 1923, and modern quantum mechanics (both wave mechanics and matrix mechanics) emerged from this insight ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).

## Context & Application

Wave-particle duality was one of the key observations that motivated the development of quantum mechanics. Thomas Young's double-slit experiment (1803) established the wave nature of light. Einstein's 1905 photoelectric effect paper showed that light also behaves as particles (photons). De Broglie's 1923 matter wave hypothesis extended duality to all matter. The synthesis of wave mechanics (Schrodinger) and matrix mechanics (Heisenberg) through Dirac's transformation theory showed that these are mathematically equivalent formulations of the same underlying theory ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)): In the double-slit experiment, light passing through two slits produces bright and dark bands (wave interference), yet is detected as individual particle hits at the screen. Placing detectors at the slits to determine which slit each photon passes through destroys the interference pattern.

**Example 2** ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)): Electrons, atoms, and molecules fired towards a double slit all exhibit the same dual behavior as light, producing interference patterns when the path is not detected.

## Relationships

- **Related to quantum-superposition** -- the interference pattern in the double-slit experiment arises because the particle is in a superposition of passing through both slits.
- **Related to wave-function** -- the wave function formalism mathematically encodes wave-particle duality, with probability amplitudes exhibiting wave-like interference.
- **Related to quantum-mechanics-theory** -- wave-particle duality is a foundational feature of quantum mechanics.

## Common Confusions

- **Duality does not mean the particle "chooses" to be a wave or particle** -- the quantum entity is always described by a wave function; what we observe depends on the measurement.
- **Wave-particle duality is not unique to light** -- it applies to all quantum entities including electrons and even large molecules.
