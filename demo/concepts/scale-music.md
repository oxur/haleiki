## Quick Definition

A musical scale is an ordered set of pitches arranged in ascending or descending order, typically spanning an octave. Scales provide the melodic and harmonic raw material for music composition and performance.

## Core Definition

Notes can be arranged in a variety of scales and modes. Western music theory generally divides the octave into a series of twelve pitches, called a chromatic scale, within which the interval between adjacent tones is called a semitone, or half step. Selecting tones from this set of 12 and arranging them in patterns of semitones and whole tones creates other scales ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The most commonly encountered scales are the seven-toned major, the harmonic minor, the melodic minor, and the natural minor. Other examples include the octatonic scale and the pentatonic or five-tone scale, which is common in folk music and blues ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Key Properties

1. Scales are ordered sequences of pitches typically spanning an octave
2. The chromatic scale contains all twelve pitches; other scales select subsets
3. The intervallic pattern (arrangement of whole steps and half steps) defines each scale type
4. The major scale follows the pattern W-W-H-W-W-W-H
5. Non-Western cultures often use scales that do not correspond to the twelve-tone division
6. Key signatures indicate the scale used for a composition

## Context & Application

Scales are fundamental to musical organization worldwide, though different cultures use very different scale systems. The Western equal temperament system divides the octave into twelve equal semitones, but classical Ottoman, Persian, Indian, and Arabic musical systems often use quarter tones and other divisions not found in Western music ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The circle of fifths conveniently shows the interrelationship of keys most commonly used in Western tonal music.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): The Ionian mode or major scale on C demonstrates the whole and half step pattern using all natural (white-key) notes: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C.

**Example 2** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): The pentatonic (five-tone) scale is common in folk music and blues, demonstrating that different numbers of tones can form viable scales.
