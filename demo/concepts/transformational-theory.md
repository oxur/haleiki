## Quick Definition

Transformational theory is a branch of music theory developed by David Lewin that models musical transformations as elements of a mathematical group. It shifts analytical focus from musical objects to the relations and transformations between them.

## Core Definition

Transformational theory is a branch of music theory developed by David Lewin in the 1980s, and formally introduced in his 1987 work, Generalized Musical Intervals and Transformations. The theory, which models musical transformations as elements of a mathematical group, can be used to analyze both tonal and atonal music ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The goal of transformational theory is to change the focus from musical objects -- such as the "C major chord" or "G major chord" -- to relations between objects. Instead of saying that a C major chord is followed by G major, a transformational theorist might say that the first chord has been "transformed" into the second by the "Dominant operation" ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The theory can be applied to pitch, rhythm and metre, or even timbre, and works for both tonal and atonal repertories ([david-lewin](/source/david-lewin/)).

## Key Properties

1. Models musical transformations as elements of mathematical groups
2. Shifts focus from musical objects to relations between objects
3. Can analyze both tonal and atonal music
4. Applicable to pitch, rhythm, metre, and timbre
5. Based on the concept of a generalized interval system (GIS)
6. Introduced formally in Lewin's 1987 Generalized Musical Intervals and Transformations

## Context & Application

Transformational theory represents the most significant application of abstract mathematics to music analysis. While traditional set theory focuses on the makeup of musical objects, transformational theory focuses on the intervals or types of musical motion that can occur. As Lewin described: "The transformational attitude does not ask for some observed measure of extension between reified 'points'; rather it asks: 'If I am at s and wish to get to t, what characteristic gesture should I perform in order to arrive there?'" ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).
