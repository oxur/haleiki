## Quick Definition

A chord is any harmonic set of three or more notes heard as if sounding simultaneously. Chords are the fundamental building blocks of harmony in Western music.

## Core Definition

A chord, in music, is any harmonic set of three or more notes that is heard as if sounding simultaneously. These need not actually be played together: arpeggios and broken chords may, for many practical and theoretical purposes, constitute chords ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The most frequently encountered chords are triads, consisting of three distinct notes. Further notes may be added to give seventh chords, extended chords, or added tone chords. The most common chords are the major and minor triads and then the augmented and diminished triads ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). A series of chords is called a chord progression, and certain patterns of chords have been accepted as establishing key in common-practice harmony ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Key Properties

1. A chord consists of three or more notes sounding simultaneously (or as if simultaneously)
2. Triads are the most common chord type, built from three notes
3. Chords are classified by quality: major, minor, augmented, diminished
4. Chords are also classified by their root note and inversion
5. Chord progressions (sequences of chords) establish key and tonal movement
6. Various notation systems exist for chords: Roman numerals, figured bass, chord letters, chord charts

## Context & Application

Chords and chord progressions are frequently used in modern Western, West African, and Oceanian music, whereas they are absent from the music of many other parts of the world ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). In the Renaissance, the sound of full triads became common for the first time ([renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)). Jazz harmony is particularly notable for its use of extended chords, tensions, and complex chord substitutions ([jazz](/source/jazz/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): The C major triad (C-E-G) is the most basic example of a major chord, consisting of a root, major third, and perfect fifth.

**Example 2** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): In popular and jazz harmony, chords are named by their root plus quality indicators, such as "C major," "D minor," or "G dominant seventh."
