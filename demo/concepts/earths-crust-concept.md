## Quick Definition

Earth's crust is the outermost solid shell of rock, comprising less than 1% of the planet's radius and volume. It exists as two distinct types: thicker, less dense continental crust and thinner, denser oceanic crust.

## Core Definition

Earth's crust is its thick outer shell of rock, the top component of the lithosphere that includes the crust and the upper part of the mantle ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)). The crust lies on top of the mantle, a configuration that is stable because the upper mantle is significantly denser than the crust. The boundary between crust and mantle is conventionally placed at the Mohorovicic discontinuity, defined by a contrast in seismic velocity ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)). Earth's crust ranges from 5 to 70 km in depth ([internal-structure-of-earth](/source/internal-structure-of-earth/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Lithosphere** — The crust is the upper part of the lithosphere; understanding this mechanical context is needed

## Key Properties

1. Two distinct types: continental (25-70 km thick, felsic) and oceanic (5-10 km thick, mafic)
2. Average thickness is about 15-20 km
3. Temperature increases with depth, reaching 700-1,600 C at the mantle boundary
4. Both types "float" on the denser mantle beneath (isostasy)
5. Oldest continental crustal rocks are 3.7-4.28 billion years old
6. Oldest oceanic crust is only about 200 million years old due to continuous recycling at subduction zones
7. Most abundant minerals are feldspars (41%), quartz (12%), and pyroxenes (11%)

## Context & Application

The crust is the only layer of Earth directly accessible for observation and sampling. Understanding its two types explains why continents stand high above ocean basins: the thicker, less dense continental crust is more buoyant ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)). Earth formed its first crust about 4.6 billion years ago, but none of that primordial crust survives today.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)): Continental crust is 25-70 km thick and composed of felsic rocks like granite, while oceanic crust is 5-10 km thick and composed of mafic rocks like basalt.

**Example 2** ([earths-crust](/source/earths-crust/)): The oldest continental crustal rocks, found in the Narryer Gneiss terrane in Western Australia and the Acasta Gneiss in Canada, are 3.7-4.28 billion years old.

## Relationships

- **Continental crust** and **oceanic crust** are the two subtypes
- **Mohorovicic discontinuity** marks its lower boundary
- **Mantle** lies beneath the crust
- **Plate tectonics** controls the creation and destruction of crust
