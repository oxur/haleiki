## Quick Definition

Tintinnabuli (from the Latin for "bells") is a compositional technique developed by Arvo Part, characterized by the pairing of a stepwise-moving melodic voice with a triadic "tintinnabuli" voice that arpeggiates or selects notes from a single triad.

## Core Definition

Tintinnabuli is a distinctive compositional technique developed by Estonian composer Arvo Part, first employed in works from 1976 onward. The technique pairs two types of voices: a melodic voice that moves stepwise (by scale steps) and a tintinnabuli voice that sounds notes of a single triad ([arvo-part](/source/arvo-part/)). The name comes from the Latin word for "bells," evoking the bell-like clarity of the resulting sonority. Part developed this technique after a period of creative silence in which he studied Gregorian chant and early polyphony.

## Key Properties

1. Pairs a stepwise melodic voice with a triadic tintinnabuli voice
2. The tintinnabuli voice selects notes exclusively from a single triad
3. Creates a stripped-down, bell-like sonority
4. Developed by Arvo Part beginning in 1976
5. Emerged from Part's study of Gregorian chant and early polyphony
6. Associated with sacred minimalism

## Context & Application

Tintinnabuli represents one of the most distinctive compositional innovations of the late 20th century. By reducing musical materials to their essence -- a scale and a triad -- Part created a style of extraordinary clarity and spiritual depth. Works like Fur Alina, Fratres, and Tabula Rasa exemplify the technique ([arvo-part](/source/arvo-part/)).
