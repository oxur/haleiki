## Quick Definition

Dualism is the philosophical position that mind and body (or matter) are fundamentally distinct. Substance dualists hold the mind is an independently existing non-physical substance, while property dualists maintain that mental properties emerge from but cannot be reduced to physical properties.

## Core Definition

Dualism is a set of views about the relationship between mind and matter that begins with the claim that mental phenomena are, in some respects, non-physical ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Substance dualists like Descartes argue that the mind is an independently existing substance -- a "res cogitans" (thinking thing) that is non-extended and non-physical. Property dualists maintain that the mind is a group of independent properties that emerge from and cannot be reduced to the brain, but that it is not a distinct substance ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Descartes was the first to formulate the mind-body problem in the form in which it still exists today.

## Prerequisites

- **Mind-body problem** — Dualism is one of the two central approaches to this problem

## Key Properties

1. Central claim: mental phenomena are in some respects non-physical
2. Substance dualism (Descartes): mind is an independently existing non-physical substance
3. Property dualism: mental properties emerge from but cannot be reduced to physical properties
4. Sub-varieties include interactionism, parallelism, occasionalism, epiphenomenalism, and panpsychism
5. Faces the "interaction problem": how can non-physical mind cause physical effects?

## Context & Application

While dualism dominated Western philosophy from Descartes through the early modern period, most contemporary philosophers of mind adopt physicalist positions. However, dualism remains influential through property dualist variants like emergent materialism and through the continued force of arguments from qualia and consciousness. Experiential dualism is accepted as the conceptual framework of Madhyamaka Buddhism ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): Descartes argued that he has a clear and distinct idea of his mind as a thinking, non-extended thing, and of his body as an extended, non-thinking thing, concluding they must be different substances.

**Example 2** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): David Chalmers' zombie argument suggests that a being physically identical to a human but lacking consciousness is conceivable, supporting property dualism.

## Relationships

- **Physicalism** is the contrasting position, holding that only physical entities exist
- **Qualia** are often cited as evidence for dualism
- **Consciousness** is the mental phenomenon dualism seeks to protect from physical reduction
- **Mind-body problem** is the question dualism attempts to answer
