## Quick Definition

Terma ("hidden treasure") are concealed Buddhist teachings, particularly those attributed to Padmasambhava, that are believed to be discovered by fortunate beings called tertöns ("treasure finders") when conditions are ripe for their reception.

## Core Definition

In Tibetan Buddhism, the teachings of Padmasambhava include an oral lineage (kama) and a lineage of the hidden treasure texts (termas). Tibetan Buddhism holds that Padmasambhava's termas are discovered by fortunate beings and tertöns when conditions are ripe for their reception ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Many of the Nyingma school's terma texts are said to have originated from the activities of Padmasambhava and his students. These hidden treasure texts are believed to be discovered and disseminated when conditions are ripe ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Padmasambhava hid numerous termas in Tibet with the aid of Yeshe Tsogyal, while she compiled and elicited his teachings ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Padmasambhava** -- the terma tradition is fundamentally connected to Padmasambhava, who is believed to have concealed the treasure texts. Understanding his role and significance is necessary context.

## Key Properties

1. **Concealed by Padmasambhava** -- termas were hidden by Padmasambhava (often with the help of Yeshe Tsogyal) throughout Tibet for future discovery.
2. **Discovered by tertöns** -- tertöns are treasure finders who discover termas when the time is right; Padmasambhava is said to appear to them in visionary encounters ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
3. **Time-appropriate** -- termas are revealed when conditions are ripe, making them relevant to the era of their discovery.
4. **Eight manifestations tradition** -- the eight manifestations of Padmasambhava belong to the terma tradition of Revealed Treasures ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
5. **Cross-school influence** -- terma cycles related to Padmasambhava gave rise to New Bon and have been practiced by figures from the Karmapa and Sakya lineages ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Context & Application

Important tertöns who contributed to the Padmasambhava tradition include Nyangral Nyima Ozer (1124-1192), who wrote the first full biography of Padmasambhava (The Copper Palace); Guru Chöwang (1212-1270); and Orgyen Lingpa (1323-c.1360), who composed the extensive Lotus Testament ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). According to tradition, the Bardo Thodol (Tibetan Book of the Dead) was among the hidden treasures subsequently discovered by Karma Lingpa (1326-1386) ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): The Bardo Thodol (commonly called the Tibetan Book of the Dead) is traditionally believed to be a terma text concealed by Padmasambhava and later discovered by Karma Lingpa.

**Example 2** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): Nyangral Nyima Ozer's Copper Palace, the first full biography of Padmasambhava, is itself a terma text said to have been revealed by him.

**Example 3** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): Padmasambhava hid many treasures in Membartsho, the burning lake in Bumthang, Bhutan.

## Relationships

- **Prerequisite: padmasambhava-teacher** -- the terma tradition is inseparable from Padmasambhava, who concealed the teachings.
- **Related to nyingma** -- the Nyingma school's teachings are heavily based on terma texts.
- **Related to dzogchen** -- many Dzogchen teachings are transmitted through the terma tradition.
