## Quick Definition

A lichen is a composite organism formed by a mutualistic symbiosis of algae or cyanobacteria living among filaments of multiple fungus species, along with embedded bacteria. Lichens are not plants, though they produce their own energy by photosynthesis.

## Core Definition

A lichen is a hybrid colony of algae or cyanobacteria living symbiotically among filaments of multiple fungus species, along with bacteria embedded in the cortex, in a mutualistic relationship ([lichen](/source/lichen/)). Lichens are the lifeform that first brought the term symbiosis into biological context. They have properties different from those of their component organisms and come in many colors, sizes, and forms ([lichen](/source/lichen/)). There are about 20,000 known species. Over 90% of plants are dependent on mycorrhizal symbiosis between plants and fungi, and lichens represent a different mode of fungal symbiosis ([fungus](/source/fungus/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Fungi** — The fungal component (mycobiont) provides the structural framework of the lichen

## Key Properties

1. Composite organism: fungus + algae or cyanobacteria + bacteria
2. The fungal component (mycobiont) provides structure; the photosynthetic partner (photobiont) provides energy
3. Growth forms include fruticose (shrubby), foliose (leafy), crustose (crust-like), squamulose, leprose, and gelatinous
4. About 20,000 known species
5. Among the first living things to grow on fresh rock (pioneer species)
6. Some are among the oldest living things
7. Can grow in extreme environments: arctic tundra, hot deserts, rocky coasts, even inside solid rock (endolithic)
8. Important in nutrient cycling and as food sources for many organisms

## Context & Application

Lichens are keystone species in many ecosystems. Their ability to colonize bare rock makes them important pioneer organisms after geological events. Their slow, regular growth rate enables lichenometry, a dating technique. Common names for lichens sometimes include "moss" (e.g., "reindeer moss"), which causes confusion with true mosses ([lichen](/source/lichen/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([lichen](/source/lichen/)): Letharia vulpina (wolf lichen) is a fruticose lichen that grows like a multiple-branched tuft; Flavoparmelia caperata is a foliose lichen with leaf-like structures; Caloplaca marina is a crustose lichen growing like an orange crust on rock.

## Relationships

- **Fungi** provide the structural framework of lichens; the fungal component is called the mycobiont
- **Moss** is often confused with lichens but is unrelated; lichens are fungal composites, mosses are plants
- **Mycorrhiza** represents a different mode of fungal symbiosis (with plant roots rather than with algae)
- **Symbiosis** is the broader concept; lichens were the first organisms to popularize the term

## Common Confusions

- **Lichens are not plants**: Despite performing photosynthesis via their algal component, lichens are primarily fungal organisms
- **Lichens are not mosses**: Common names like "reindeer moss" and "Iceland moss" are misleading; lichens are fungal symbioses, not related to mosses
