## Quick Definition

The Nyingma ("Ancient Ones") is the oldest school of Tibetan Buddhism, tracing its origins to the teachings of Padmasambhava in the 8th century. It is distinguished by its preservation of the earliest translations of Buddhist texts into Tibetan and its emphasis on Dzogchen as the highest teaching.

## Core Definition

The contemporary Nyingma school considers Padmasambhava to be a founding figure ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Because of his role in the founding of Samye monastery, the first monastery in Tibet, Padmasambhava is regarded as the founder of the Nyingma school ("Ancients") of Tibetan Buddhism ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). The Nyingma school also traditionally holds that its Dzogchen lineage has its origins in Garab Dorje through a lineage of transmission to Padmasambhava ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Longchenpa was one of the most brilliant teachers of the Nyingma lineage, who systematized the Nyingma teachings in his Seven Treasuries and wrote extensively on Dzogchen ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Vajrayana** -- the Nyingma school is a Vajrayana Buddhist tradition; understanding the Vajrayana framework of tantric practices and transmission lineages is necessary context.

## Key Properties

1. **Oldest school** -- the Nyingma is the earliest of the four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism, dating to the 8th century.
2. **Founded by Padmasambhava** -- Padmasambhava's establishment of Samye monastery and his teaching activities in Tibet are regarded as the school's founding.
3. **Dzogchen emphasis** -- the Nyingma tradition places special emphasis on Dzogchen (Great Perfection) as the highest teaching.
4. **Terma tradition** -- many Nyingma teachings are transmitted through terma (hidden treasure texts) attributed to Padmasambhava and revealed by later tertöns ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
5. **Oral and treasure lineages** -- the school preserves both kama (oral transmission) and terma lineages.
6. **Systematized by Longchenpa** -- Longchenpa's Seven Treasuries provided a comprehensive systematization of Nyingma teachings ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Context & Application

The Nyingma school preserves the earliest translations of Buddhist texts into Tibetan, completed during the "Early Translation" period under Padmasambhava, Shantarakshita, and King Trisong Detsen. Padmasambhava and his students created the Tibetan canon by translating all of the Buddha's teachings and commentaries into Tibetan ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). The school's influence extends beyond its own adherents: tantric cycles related to Padmasambhava have been practiced by prominent figures of other Tibetan Buddhist schools, including the Karmapas and Sakya lineage heads ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). In the 14th century, Longchenpa brought coherence to the Nyingma teachings through his extensive writings, making him one of the most important figures in the lineage after Padmasambhava himself ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): Samye Monastery, designed by Padmasambhava during the reign of King Trisong Detsen, was the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet and the founding institution of the Nyingma school.

**Example 2** ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)): Longchenpa's Seven Treasuries systematized the Nyingma teachings, written to elucidate the meaning of the seventeen main tantras of Dzogchen and the teachings of all nine yanas.

**Example 3** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): Among Padmasambhava's twenty-five main students, sixteen women practitioners became accomplished Nyingma masters during the Early Translation period.
