## Quick Definition

The Nyingma ("Ancient Ones") is the oldest of the four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism, tracing its origins to the earliest translations of Buddhist scriptures into Tibetan in the 8th century under Padmasambhava, Shantarakshita, and King Trisong Detsen. It is distinguished by its Nine Yanas classification system, its emphasis on Dzogchen as the highest teaching, and its terma (hidden treasure text) tradition.

## Core Definition

The Nyingma school was founded by Padmasambhava as the first translations of Buddhist scriptures from Pali and Sanskrit into Tibetan occurred in the eighth century ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The establishment of Tibetan Buddhism and the Nyingma tradition is collectively ascribed to Khenpo Shantarakshita, Guru Padmasambhava, and King Trisong Detsen, known as Khen Lop Chos Sum ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The Nyingma tradition traces its Dzogchen lineage from the first Buddha Samantabhadra to Garab Dorje, and its other lineages from Indian mahasiddhas such as Sri Singha and Jnanasutra ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The Nyingma tradition was physically founded at Samye, the first monastery in Tibet ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Longchenpa was one of the most brilliant teachers of the Nyingma lineage, who systematized the Nyingma teachings in his Seven Treasuries and wrote extensively on Dzogchen ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)). The contemporary Nyingma school considers Padmasambhava to be a founding figure ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Vajrayana** -- the Nyingma school is a Vajrayana Buddhist tradition; understanding the Vajrayana framework of tantric practices and transmission lineages is necessary context.

## Key Properties

1. **Oldest school** -- the Nyingma is the earliest of the four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism, dating to the 8th century.
2. **Founded by Padmasambhava** -- Padmasambhava's establishment of Samye monastery and his teaching activities in Tibet are regarded as the school's founding.
3. **Dzogchen emphasis** -- the Nyingma tradition places special emphasis on Dzogchen (Great Perfection) as the highest teaching; the main Dzogchen sources like the Seventeen Tantras are seen as communicating a path that goes beyond Highest Yoga Tantra ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
4. **Nine Yanas classification** -- the Nyingma school uses a unique doxography dividing the Buddhist path into nine vehicles: three sutra vehicles, three outer tantra vehicles, and three inner tantra vehicles (Mahayoga, Anuyoga, Atiyoga/Dzogchen) ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
5. **Terma tradition** -- many Nyingma teachings are transmitted through terma (hidden treasure texts) attributed to Padmasambhava and revealed by later tertons ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
6. **Dual transmission lineages** -- the school preserves both kama (oral transmission) and terma (hidden treasure) lineages ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
7. **Systematized by Longchenpa** -- Longchenpa's Seven Treasuries provided a comprehensive systematization of Nyingma teachings ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).
8. **Decentralized governance** -- unlike other Tibetan Buddhist schools, the Nyingma traditionally had no centralized authority or single head of lineage; governance was by community of lamas and senior sangha ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
9. **Dual sangha** -- the Nyingma has a "red sangha" of ordained monks and nuns and a "white sangha" of vow-holding ngakmapas who can be householders ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
10. **Six Mother Monasteries** -- the principal Nyingma institutions are the six mother monasteries: Katok, Dorje Drak, Palyul, Mindrolling, Dzogchen, and Shechen ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).

## Context & Application

The Nyingma school preserves the earliest translations of Buddhist texts into Tibetan, completed during the "Early Translation" period under Padmasambhava, Shantarakshita, and King Trisong Detsen. Padmasambhava supervised mainly the translation of tantras while Shantarakshita concentrated on the sutras; together they founded Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). From the eighth until the eleventh century, this textual tradition was the only form of Buddhism in Tibet ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Buddhism was persecuted under King Langdarma (836-842), forcing it largely underground for 300 years ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The "new translation" (Sarma) schools emerged from the 11th century onward, and the term "Nyingma" came into usage to distinguish those who continued to use the "old" translations ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). In the 14th century, Longchenpa brought coherence to the Nyingma teachings through his extensive writings ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)). Jigme Lingpa's Longchen Nyingthig (18th century) became one of the most widely used Nyingma Dzogchen teachings ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The 19th century saw the rise of the non-sectarian Rime movement, led by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo and Jamgon Kongtrul, which sought to preserve Kagyu, Sakya, and Nyingma teachings ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Mipham the Great's writings in the late 19th century "completely revolutionised Nyingma pa scholasticism" ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)): Samye Monastery, designed by Padmasambhava and Shantarakshita during the reign of King Trisong Detsen (founded 775-779), was the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet and the founding institution of the Nyingma school.

**Example 2** ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)): Longchenpa's Seven Treasuries systematized the Nyingma teachings, written to elucidate the meaning of the seventeen main tantras of Dzogchen and the teachings of all nine yanas.

**Example 3** ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)): The Rinchen Terdzod, assembled by Jamgon Kongtrul at the request of Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo in the 19th century, is the most important collection of terma treasure texts for Nyingmapas, comprising thousands of texts from across Tibet.

**Example 4** ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)): The Guhyagarbha Tantra is the foremost of the eighteen great tantras at the heart of the Nyingma Mahayoga tradition; it abridges the content of the seventeen others.

## Common Confusions

- **Nyingma as "less authentic"** -- the Sarma schools challenged the Indic origins of some Nyingma texts; however, Sanskrit originals of some texts previously questioned have been discovered in Nepal ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
- **Nyingma as homogeneous** -- the Nyingma is highly decentralized; individual monasteries and lineages maintain distinct traditions and administrations ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).
