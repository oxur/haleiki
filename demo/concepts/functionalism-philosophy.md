## Quick Definition

Functionalism is the view that mental states are defined by their causal relations with other mental states, sensory inputs, and behavioral outputs, rather than by their physical constitution. This means that any system with the right functional organization could have mental states, regardless of its material makeup.

## Core Definition

Functionalism was formulated by Hilary Putnam and Jerry Fodor as a reaction to the inadequacies of the identity theory. Mental states are characterized by their causal relations with other mental states and with sensory inputs and behavioral outputs. Functionalism abstracts away from the details of the physical implementation of a mental state by characterizing it in terms of non-mental functional properties ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Many cognitive scientists hold a functionalist view of the mind, and according to the multiple realizability account, even non-human systems such as robots and computers can be ascribed as having cognitive states ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Mind-body problem** — Functionalism is a response to this problem
- **Physicalism** — Functionalism is a variant of physicalist monism

## Key Properties

1. Mental states defined by functional (causal) roles, not physical substrate
2. Supports multiple realizability: the same mental state can be implemented in different physical systems
3. Abstracted from physical implementation details
4. Key to the computational theory of mind
5. Provides philosophical basis for artificial intelligence research

## Context & Application

Functionalism bridges philosophy of mind and AI by suggesting that mental states are substrate-independent. If a computer implements the right functional organization, it could in principle have mental states. David Chalmers uses functionalist reasoning in his "fading qualia" and "dancing qualia" thought experiments about artificial consciousness ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): A kidney is characterized scientifically by its functional role in filtering blood, not by its specific cellular composition; similarly, functionalism characterizes mental states by their causal roles.

## Relationships

- **Physicalism** is the broader category; functionalism is a physicalist approach
- **Cognitive science** adopts functionalism as a working assumption for many researchers
- **Artificial consciousness** research builds on functionalist premises about substrate independence
- **Dualism** is the contrasting view that mental states cannot be reduced to functional roles
