## Quick Definition

The Rime (ris med, "non-sectarian") movement was a 19th-century Tibetan Buddhist movement led by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo and Jamgon Kongtrul that sought to collect, preserve, and print the teachings of the Sakya, Kagyu, and Nyingma schools in response to the hegemonic influence of the Gelug school.

## Core Definition

The 19th century saw the rise of the non-sectarian Rime movement, led by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo (1820-1892) and Jamgon Kongtrul (1813-1899), which sought to collect and print the teachings of the Sakya, Kagyu, and Nyingma schools in response to the hegemonic influence of the Gelug school ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Kongtrul and his colleagues worked together to compile, exchange, and revive the teachings of these schools, including many near-extinct teachings. The movement held that there was value in all Buddhist traditions, and all were worthy of study and preservation ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)). According to Sam van Schaik, without this collecting and printing of rare works, the later suppression of Buddhism by the Communists would have been much more final ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)).

- **Nyingma** -- the Rime movement arose partly in response to challenges facing the Nyingma school; understanding the Nyingma tradition provides key context.
- **Kagyu** -- the Kagyu school was a primary partner in the Rime movement.

## Key Properties

1. **Non-sectarian preservation** -- the movement compiled, exchanged, and revived teachings from multiple schools that were in danger of being lost ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)).
2. **Key figures** -- primarily Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo, Jamgon Kongtrul, Chogyur Lingpa, and Mipham Gyatso ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)).
3. **Five Great Treasuries** -- Jamgon Kongtrul compiled the Five Great Treasuries, including the Rinchen Terdzod and the Treasury of Knowledge ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)).
4. **Response to Gelug hegemony** -- arose in the context of political and inter-religious conflict in eastern Tibet ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)).

## Context & Application

The Rime movement was centered in Kham, eastern Tibet, and was particularly associated with Palpung monastery. Jamgon Kongtrul's personal hermitage became an important center for three-year retreats and the composition of major works ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)). The Rinchen Terdzod, assembled by Kongtrul, is the most important collection of terma treasure texts for Nyingmapas ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The movement's influence extended into the 20th century through figures like Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche, who is considered an eminent proponent of the Rime tradition.
