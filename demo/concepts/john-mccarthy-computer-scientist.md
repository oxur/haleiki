## Quick Definition

John McCarthy (1927-2011) was an American computer scientist who coined the term "artificial intelligence," invented the Lisp programming language, pioneered time-sharing, and created garbage collection for automatic memory management.

## Core Definition

John McCarthy was one of the founders of the discipline of artificial intelligence. He co-authored the proposal for the Dartmouth workshop which coined the term "artificial intelligence," led the development of the Lisp programming language family, popularized time-sharing, and invented garbage collection ([john-mccarthy](/source/john-mccarthy/)). He received the 1971 Turing Award for his contributions to AI. McCarthy spent most of his career at Stanford University, where he helped establish the Stanford AI Laboratory ([john-mccarthy](/source/john-mccarthy/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Lisp inventor** -- created Lisp beginning in 1958, publishing the foundational paper "Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions" in 1960.
2. **AI founder** -- co-coined the term "artificial intelligence" for the 1956 Dartmouth conference with Minsky, Rochester, and Shannon.
3. **Time-sharing pioneer** -- instrumental in creating three of the earliest time-sharing systems (CTSS, BBN, Dartmouth).
4. **Garbage collection inventor** -- invented automatic memory management around 1959 to solve Lisp memory problems.
5. **Circumscription** -- developed the circumscription method of non-monotonic reasoning (1978-1986).
6. **AI philosophy** -- championed logic-based AI, arguing that machines could have beliefs and that every aspect of human intelligence could be formalized.

## Context & Application

McCarthy was born in Boston in 1927, earned his PhD in mathematics from Princeton, and held positions at Dartmouth, MIT, and Stanford. His von Neumann lecture at Caltech inspired his computational work. At MIT, he helped motivate Project MAC. He advised 30 PhD graduates including Barbara Liskov and Raj Reddy. His 1961 utility computing speech anticipated cloud computing by decades ([john-mccarthy](/source/john-mccarthy/)). Lisp, his most enduring creation, became the standard language for AI research and influenced dozens of subsequent languages ([lisp-language](/source/lisp-language/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([john-mccarthy](/source/john-mccarthy/)): McCarthy proposed the advice taker in 1958, which inspired later work on question-answering and logic programming.

**Example 2** ([lisp-language](/source/lisp-language/)): Steve Russell implemented the first Lisp interpreter by compiling McCarthy's eval function into IBM 704 machine code, to McCarthy's surprise: "I said to him, ho, ho, you're confusing theory with practice."
