## Quick Definition

Longchenpa (1308-1364), also known as Longchen Rabjam ("Infinite, Vast Expanse of Space"), was one of the most brilliant teachers and writers of the Nyingma lineage. He systematized the Nyingma teachings in his Seven Treasuries and wrote extensively on Dzogchen.

## Core Definition

Longchenpa, also known as Longchen Rabjam, was one of the most brilliant teachers of the Nyingma lineage. He systematized the Nyingma teachings in his Seven Treasuries and wrote extensively on Dzogchen. He transmitted the Longchen Nyingtik cycle of teachings and practice to Jikme Lingpa, and it has since become one of the most widely practiced traditions ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)). The figure of Longchen Rabjam stands out as one of the greatest Dzogchen masters in the Nyingma tradition and amongst the most brilliant and original writers in Tibetan Buddhist literature. He was the author of over 200 works, of which only about twenty-five survive ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Systematizer** -- brought coherence to the Nyingma Dzogchen teachings, synthesizing the Vima Nyingtik and Khandro Nyingtik in the Nyingtik Yabshi ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).
2. **Seven Treasuries** -- his most famous works, written to elucidate the seventeen main tantras of Dzogchen and the teachings of all nine yanas.
3. **Prolific writer** -- authored over 200 works, of which about 25 survive, including the Seven Treasuries and Three Trilogies ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).
4. **Lineage holder** -- received transmissions from Rigdzin Kumaradza and the Third Karmapa, Rangjung Dorje, bridging multiple traditions ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).
5. **Visionary connection** -- had a decisive vision of Padmasambhava and Yeshe Tsogyal that shaped his spiritual development ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).
6. **Indirect incarnation** -- regarded as the immediate reincarnation of Pema Ledreltsal and an indirect incarnation of the princess Pema Sal ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Context & Application

Longchenpa was born in 1308 in the Tra Valley of Southern Tibet. He was ordained at age eleven, studied extensively at the famous Sangphu Neuthok shedra, and received transmissions from teachers across multiple Tibetan Buddhist schools. His meeting with Rigdzin Kumaradza during his late twenties was of decisive importance: he accompanied the master for two years in difficult mountain conditions, receiving all his transmissions ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)). After years in retreat, he attracted students and founded monasteries in Bhutan, including Tharpaling near Bumthang. He composed many of his writings at Gangri Thokar. Longchenpa passed away in 1364, and his legacy has been described as making him one of the most important figures in the Nyingma tradition ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)): Longchenpa used different names in the colophons of his writings depending on subject matter: Dorje Ziji for outer and inner tantras, Drime Ozer for meditation stages, and Longchen Rabjam for the inconceivable nature.

**Example 2** ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)): His parinirvana poem includes: "My delight in death is far, far greater than / The delight of traders at making vast fortunes at sea."
