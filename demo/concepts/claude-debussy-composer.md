## Quick Definition

Claude Debussy (1862-1918) was a French composer who developed an innovative style of harmony and orchestral coloring. Sometimes called the first Impressionist composer, he was among the most influential composers of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Core Definition

Achille Claude Debussy was a French composer sometimes seen as the first Impressionist composer, although he vigorously rejected the term. He was among the most influential composers of the late 19th and early 20th centuries ([claude-debussy](/source/claude-debussy/)). His music was to a considerable extent a reaction against Wagner and the German musical tradition. With early influences including Russian and Far Eastern music and works by Chopin, Debussy developed his own style of harmony and orchestral colouring ([claude-debussy](/source/claude-debussy/)). His works strongly influenced a wide range of composers including Bartok, Stravinsky, Gershwin, Messiaen, and the jazz pianist Bill Evans.

## Key Properties

1. Born in Saint-Germain-en-Laye (1862), died in Paris (1918)
2. Developed innovative harmony drawing on Russian, Far Eastern, and Impressionist influences
3. Only completed opera: Pelleas et Melisande (1902)
4. Major orchestral works: Prelude a l'apres-midi d'un faune, La mer, Nocturnes
5. Rejected the "Impressionist" label applied to his music
6. Strongly influenced Messiaen, Stravinsky, and jazz harmony through Bill Evans

## Context & Application

Debussy's harmonic innovations challenged the dominant German Romantic tradition, treating dissonance as a legitimate sonority rather than something requiring resolution. His use of whole-tone scales, parallel chord movement, and non-functional harmony opened new possibilities that influenced both classical and jazz traditions ([claude-debussy](/source/claude-debussy/)). In a music-theory context, a major second may sound stable and consonant in a Debussy prelude, while the same interval may sound dissonant in a Bach fugue ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).
