## Quick Definition

Gregorian chant is a monophonic, unaccompanied sacred vocal form that represents the earliest known music of the Christian church. Named after Pope Gregory I, it was the dominant musical form of the Western medieval period.

## Core Definition

Chant (or plainsong) is a monophonic sacred form (single, unaccompanied melody) which represents the earliest known music of the Christian church ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). Chant developed separately in several European centres, including Rome, Hispania, Gaul, Milan, and Ireland. Gregorian chant became the dominant form after Charlemagne's standardization efforts in the 9th century. By the 12th and 13th centuries, Gregorian chant had superseded all the other Western chant traditions, with the exception of the Ambrosian chant in Milan and the Mozarabic chant in a few specially designated Spanish chapels ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)).

## Key Properties

1. Monophonic: consists of a single melodic line without accompaniment
2. Sacred: composed for liturgical use in the Catholic Church
3. Modal: organized according to the system of eight church modes
4. Textual: closely aligned with Latin liturgical texts
5. Named after Pope Gregory I, though the attribution is largely legendary
6. Transmitted initially by oral tradition, later through neumatic notation

## Context & Application

Gregorian chant was the foundation upon which medieval Western music was built. The development of musical notation was driven by the need to standardize and transmit chant across the Carolingian Empire ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). Chant also served as the basis for early polyphony: organum began as the addition of a second voice to existing chant melodies ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)): The Kyrie Eleison XI (Orbis Factor) from the Liber Usualis is a representative example of Gregorian chant, with neumatic notation indicating the melodic contour above the liturgical text.

**Example 2** ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)): Hildegard von Bingen (1098-1179) was one of the earliest known female composers, writing many monophonic works for the Catholic Church, almost all for female voices.

## Relationships

- **mode-music** -- Gregorian chant is organized according to the eight church modes
- **monophony** -- Gregorian chant is the paramount example of monophonic musical texture
- **organum** -- Organum evolved from the practice of adding voices to existing chant melodies
- **polyphony** -- The development of polyphony began as an elaboration of Gregorian chant
