## Quick Definition

Ectomycorrhiza (EcM) is a type of mycorrhizal association in which fungi form a sheath (mantle) around root tips and a network of hyphae (Hartig net) between root cortex cells, without penetrating the cells themselves. It associates primarily with woody plants and boasts very high fungal species diversity.

## Core Definition

Ectomycorrhizas are distinct in that they do not penetrate into plant cells, but instead form a structure called a Hartig net that penetrates between cells. They consist of a hyphal sheath (mantle) covering the root tip and the Hartig net of hyphae surrounding plant cells within the root cortex ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)). EcM are symbiotic associations between the roots of around 10% of plant families, mostly woody plants including birch, eucalyptus, oak, and pine families, and fungi belonging to Basidiomycota, Ascomycota, and Zygomycota. While EcM associate with relatively few plant species (~2%), those species are mostly dominant trees making up a large proportion of plant biomass ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Mycorrhiza** — EcM is one of the two major subtypes

## Key Properties

1. Intercellular: hyphae penetrate between cells (Hartig net) but not into them
2. Form a hyphal mantle/sheath around root tips
3. Associate with ~2% of plant species, but these are ecologically dominant trees
4. Very high fungal diversity: estimated 7,750-25,000 species
5. Evolved from free-living saprotrophs, mostly in Basidiomycota and Ascomycota
6. Developed substantially later than AM (Jurassic period)
7. Some species are host-specific (Leccinum, Suillus); others are generalists (Amanita)
8. Nutrients can move between plants through ectomycorrhizal networks

## Context & Application

Ectomycorrhizal networks connect trees in forests, enabling transfer of nutrients between individual plants. Carbon has been shown to move between paper birch and Douglas-fir seedlings through these networks. The ectomycorrhizal fungus Laccaria bicolor can lure and kill springtails to obtain nitrogen for its host plant ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)): An individual tree may have 15 or more different ectomycorrhizal fungal partners at one time.

**Example 2** ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)): Laccaria bicolor, the first EcM fungus to have its genome sequenced (2008), was found to lack enzymes for degrading plant cell walls, preventing it from harming host cells during colonization.

## Relationships

- **Arbuscular mycorrhiza** contrasts as the intracellular type with much lower fungal diversity but higher plant diversity
- **Mycorrhiza** is the broader concept
- **Fungi** of multiple phyla independently evolved EcM lifestyles (convergent evolution)
