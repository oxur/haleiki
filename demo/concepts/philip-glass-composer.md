## Quick Definition

Philip Glass (b. 1937) is an American composer known for his minimalist style characterized by repetitive structures, additive processes, and a blend of classical and popular music idioms. He is one of the most influential and commercially successful composers of the late 20th century.

## Core Definition

Philip Glass is an American composer born in Baltimore, Maryland, in 1937. He studied at the Peabody Institute, the University of Chicago, and the Juilliard School, and is known for his work in minimal music ([philip-glass](/source/philip-glass/)). Glass founded the Philip Glass Ensemble and has composed prolifically across opera, symphony, chamber music, and film scores. His style is characterized by repetitive, cyclical structures and additive rhythmic processes.

## Context & Application

Glass's minimalism emerged in the mid-1960s, influenced by his studies with Nadia Boulanger in Paris and encounters with Indian music through Ravi Shankar. His "portrait opera" trilogy -- Einstein on the Beach (1976), Satyagraha (1979), and Akhnaten (1983) -- brought minimalist techniques to the operatic stage ([philip-glass](/source/philip-glass/)).
