## Quick Definition

Classical Tibetan is a liturgical language of Tibetan Buddhism dating from the 9th century CE, developed from Old Tibetan and standardized for translating Sanskrit Buddhist canonical texts. It remains a living classical language used in religious contexts.

## Core Definition

Classical Tibetan is a liturgical language of Tibetan Buddhism that dates from the 9th century, particularly referring to the language of early canonical texts translated from other languages, especially Sanskrit. Standardization occurred in 816 CE during the reign of King Sadnalegs. It is one of the handful of "living" classical languages alongside Arabic, Ge'ez, and New Persian, though it meaningfully differs from Modern Standard Tibetan ([classical-tibetan](/source/classical-tibetan/)). It belongs to the Sino-Tibetan language family, in the Tibeto-Kanauri > Bodish > Tibetic branch.

## Key Properties

1. **Liturgical function** -- serves as the liturgical language of Tibetan Buddhism.
2. **Sanskrit translation medium** -- developed primarily to translate Sanskrit Buddhist texts into Tibetan.
3. **816 CE standardization** -- standardized during King Sadnalegs's reign.
4. **Tibetan script** -- written in the Tibetan script (an abugida).
5. **Distinct from modern Tibetan** -- differs from Modern Standard Tibetan due to centuries of divergence.
6. **Complex phonology** -- the orthography preserves an archaic phonological system similar to Old Tibetan.

## Context & Application

Old Tibetan was used from the 7th century to translate Sanskrit Buddhist texts, and the standardization gave rise to Classical Tibetan. The grammar varies depending on the period and geographic origin of the author. Some medieval writers used more colloquial language, creating the differences between Modern Literary Tibetan and Classical Tibetan that exist today ([classical-tibetan](/source/classical-tibetan/)).
