## Quick Definition

Miles Davis (1926-1991) was an American jazz trumpeter, bandleader, and composer who was at the forefront of virtually every major development in jazz from the mid-1940s onward, including cool jazz, hard bop, modal jazz, and jazz fusion.

## Core Definition

Miles Dewey Davis III was an American jazz trumpeter, bandleader, and composer whose career spanned several of the most significant stylistic developments in jazz history ([miles-davis](/source/miles-davis/)). His recordings that constitute Birth of the Cool are identified as foundational examples of the cool jazz genre ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)). Davis's solo on Charlie Parker's "Chasin' the Bird" in 1947 anticipated the Cool Era. His later work with Kind of Blue was pivotal in establishing modal jazz, while his electric period in the late 1960s-70s helped define jazz fusion ([jazz](/source/jazz/)).

4. Kind of Blue (1959) is the best-selling jazz album of all time
5. His electric period (late 1960s-70s) fused jazz with rock
6. Known for a distinctive, muted trumpet tone

## Context & Application

Davis was consistently at the vanguard of jazz innovation. His soft variants of bebop in the Birth of the Cool sessions helped establish a new aesthetic in jazz ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)). His embrace of modal improvisation with Kind of Blue altered the direction of jazz in the late 1950s. His electric period with albums like Bitches Brew bridged jazz and rock audiences ([jazz](/source/jazz/)).
