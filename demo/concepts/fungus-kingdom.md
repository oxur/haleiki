## Quick Definition

Fungi are a kingdom of eukaryotic organisms that includes yeasts, molds, and mushrooms. They are heterotrophs that obtain nutrition by absorbing dissolved organic molecules, distinguished from plants by having chitin in their cell walls and from animals by their absorptive nutrition.

## Core Definition

A fungus is any member of the group of eukaryotic organisms that includes yeasts, molds, and mushrooms, classified in the biological kingdom Fungi ([fungus](/source/fungus/)). A characteristic that places fungi in a different kingdom from plants, bacteria, and some protists is having chitin in their cell walls. Fungi, like animals, are heterotrophs: they acquire nutrition by absorbing dissolved organic molecules, typically by secreting digestive enzymes into their environment. They do not perform photosynthesis. Fungi are the principal decomposers in ecological systems ([fungus](/source/fungus/)).

## Key Properties

1. Eukaryotic organisms with membrane-bound nuclei
2. Cell walls contain chitin (unique chitin-glucan complex)
3. Heterotrophic: absorb dissolved organic molecules from the environment
4. Cannot photosynthesize (lack chloroplasts)
5. Grow as tubular, filamentous structures called hyphae or as unicellular yeasts
6. Reproduce via spores, both sexually and asexually
7. Estimated 2.2-3.8 million species, with only ~148,000 described
8. Genetically more closely related to animals than to plants
9. Over 90% of plants depend on mycorrhizal symbiosis with fungi

## Context & Application

Fungi perform an essential role in decomposition of organic matter and have fundamental roles in nutrient cycling. Over 90% of plants are dependent on mycorrhizal symbiosis between plants and fungi ([fungus](/source/fungus/)). Fungi are used in food production (bread, wine, beer, soy sauce), antibiotic production, biological pest control, and industrial enzyme production. Some produce toxic mycotoxins; others are significant pathogens of humans, animals, and plants.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([fungus](/source/fungus/)): Amanita muscaria is a well-known basidiomycete mushroom; Sarcoscypha coccinea is an ascomycete; bread mold and chytrids illustrate the range of fungal diversity.

**Example 2** ([fungus](/source/fungus/)): The fungus kingdom encompasses diversity from unicellular aquatic chytrids to large mushrooms, with a worldwide distribution including extreme environments.

## Relationships

- **Mycorrhiza** is the mutualistic symbiosis between fungi and plant roots
- **Lichen** is a symbiotic composite of fungi with algae or cyanobacteria
- **Hypha** is the basic structural unit of most fungi
- **Spore** is the primary reproductive structure

## Common Confusions

- **Fungi are not plants**: Despite historical classification as plants, fungi are genetically more closely related to animals and lack chloroplasts
- **Fungi vs. slime molds**: Myxomycetes (slime molds) and oomycetes (water molds) are structurally similar but are not true fungi
