## Quick Definition

Medieval music encompasses the sacred and secular music of Western Europe during the Middle Ages, approximately the 6th to 15th centuries. It saw the development of musical notation, the modal system, and the emergence of polyphony from monophonic chant.

## Core Definition

Medieval music encompasses the sacred and secular music of Western Europe during the Middle Ages, from approximately the 6th to 15th centuries. It is the first and longest major era of Western classical music and is followed by the Renaissance music ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). The medieval period saw the creation and adaptation of systems of music notation, the development of polyphony from monophonic chant through organum, and the establishment of the modal system as the basis for pitch organization. Music was primarily vocal, including Gregorian chant and later polyphonic genres ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)).

## Key Properties

1. Spans approximately the 6th to 15th centuries
2. First and longest major era of Western classical music
3. Divided into Early (500-1000), High (1000-1300), and Late (1300-1400) periods
4. Development of music notation from neumes to mensural notation
5. Emergence of polyphony from monophonic chant
6. Modal system governed pitch organization
7. Key genres: plainchant, organum, motet, conductus

## Context & Application

The medieval period laid the foundations for all subsequent Western music, including the development of notation, the emergence of polyphony, and the establishment of theoretical frameworks for rhythm and pitch. The only repertory surviving from before 800 is the plainsong liturgical music of the Roman Catholic Church ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)): Gregorian chant, the dominant monophonic sacred form, represents the earliest surviving body of Western music.

**Example 2** ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)): The Notre-Dame school (c. 1150-1250) produced elaborate polyphonic organum by composers Leonin and Perotin.
