## Quick Definition

An abjad is a type of alphabet whose letters represent only consonant sounds, with vowels generally left unwritten or optionally marked with diacritics. Abjads were the first alphabets to develop historically, primarily used for Semitic languages.

## Core Definition

An abjad is an alphabet whose letters only represent the consonantal sounds of a language. They were the first alphabets to develop historically, with most used to write Semitic languages, and originally deriving from the Proto-Sinaitic script. The morphology of Semitic languages is particularly suited to this approach, as the denotation of vowels is generally redundant. Optional vowel markings may be used for applications like education. Many pure alphabets were derived from abjads through the addition of dedicated vowel letters, as with the derivation of the Greek alphabet from the Phoenician alphabet c. 800 BC ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Alphabet** -- abjads are a subtype of alphabets.

## Key Properties

1. **Consonant-only** -- letters primarily represent consonant sounds.
2. **Semitic language suitability** -- well-suited to Semitic morphology where vowel patterns are predictable.
3. **Historical primacy** -- abjads are the oldest alphabetic writing systems.
4. **Optional vowel marks** -- diacritics for vowels are available but typically limited to pedagogical use.
5. **Proto-Sinaitic descent** -- most abjads descend from the Proto-Sinaitic/Proto-Canaanite script.

## Context & Application

The word "abjad" derives from the Arabic word for "alphabet," itself from the traditional order of Arabic letters (alif, ba', jim, dal). Major abjads include the Arabic alphabet, Hebrew alphabet, and historical Phoenician alphabet. The Phoenician abjad (c. 1050 BC) was the ancestor of the Greek alphabet, which added vowel letters to create the first pure alphabet ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/), [proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)): The Proto-Sinaitic script was an abjad: the character for /b/ derives from the Egyptian hieroglyph for "house," representing only the consonant.
