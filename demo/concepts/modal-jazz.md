## Quick Definition

Modal jazz is a style that developed in the late 1950s, using the musical mode or scale as the basis of musical structure and improvisation rather than chord progressions. Miles Davis's Kind of Blue is its most celebrated recording.

## Core Definition

Modal jazz developed in the late 1950s, using the mode, or musical scale, as the basis of musical structure and improvisation ([jazz](/source/jazz/)). Rather than navigating rapid chord changes as in bebop, modal jazz musicians improvise over sustained modes, allowing greater melodic freedom and a more spacious, contemplative quality. Modal jazz is listed as a derivative form of cool jazz ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)).

## Prerequisites

- **jazz** -- Modal jazz is a subgenre of jazz

## Key Properties

1. Uses modes (scales) rather than chord changes as the basis for improvisation
2. Developed in the late 1950s
3. Allows greater melodic freedom by reducing harmonic complexity
4. Creates a more spacious, contemplative quality than chord-based jazz
5. Key recording: Miles Davis's Kind of Blue (1959)
6. Derivative form of cool jazz according to some classifications

## Context & Application

Modal jazz represented a significant departure from the chord-based approach of bebop and hard bop. By simplifying the harmonic structure to sustained modes, it gave improvisers freedom to explore melodic ideas without the constraint of rapid chord changes. This approach was enormously influential on subsequent jazz styles ([jazz](/source/jazz/)).
