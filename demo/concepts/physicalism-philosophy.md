## Quick Definition

Physicalism is the philosophical position that everything that exists is physical, and that mental states and properties will ultimately be explained in terms of physical processes. It is the dominant monist approach in contemporary philosophy of mind.

## Core Definition

Physicalists argue that only entities postulated by physical theory exist, and that mental processes will eventually be explained in terms of these entities as physical theory continues to evolve ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Most modern philosophers of mind adopt either a reductive physicalist or non-reductive physicalist position, maintaining that the mind is not something separate from the body. Reductive physicalists assert that all mental states will eventually be explained by physiological processes. Non-reductive physicalists argue that mental properties supervene on physical properties but are not reducible to them ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Mind-body problem** — Physicalism is one of the two central approaches to this problem

## Key Properties

1. Central claim: only physical entities exist; mental states are physical states
2. Dominant position in contemporary philosophy of mind
3. Reductive variant: mental states are identical to brain states (type identity theory)
4. Non-reductive variant: mental properties supervene on physical properties but are not reducible
5. Major sub-positions include behaviorism, type identity theory, anomalous monism, and functionalism
6. Explicitly rejected by Buddhism, which holds mental events are no more or less real than physical events

## Context & Application

Physicalism has been particularly influential in the sciences, especially sociobiology, computer science (AI), evolutionary psychology, and neuroscience. However, the existence of qualia and the hard problem of consciousness continue to challenge physicalist accounts ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): Type identity theory (Smart and Place) holds that a mental state like "desire for coffee" is nothing other than a specific pattern of neural firing in certain brain regions.

**Example 2** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): Eliminative materialists (Patricia and Paul Churchland) argue that folk psychology's sentence-like model of cognition will be replaced by neural network models.

## Relationships

- **Dualism** is the contrasting position
- **Functionalism** is a physicalist approach defining mental states by causal roles
- **Consciousness** is the phenomenon physicalism must explain in physical terms
