## Quick Definition

An alphabet is a writing system that uses symbols called letters to represent the phonemes (individual sounds) of a language, including both consonants and vowels. It is subdivided into pure alphabets, abjads, and abugidas.

## Core Definition

An alphabet uses symbols called letters that correspond to the phonemes of a language. Alphabets are generally classified into three subtypes: pure alphabets have letters for both consonant and vowel sounds, abjads generally only use letters representing consonant sounds, and abugidas use letters representing consonant-vowel pairs. The word "alphabet" derives from alpha and beta, the names for the first two letters in the Greek alphabet. Alphabets typically use fewer than 100 distinct symbols ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/)). Alphabetic writing first appeared c. 1800 BC with the Proto-Sinaitic script, and most of the world's alphabets either descend directly from it or were inspired by its design ([proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Phoneme-level representation** -- letters correspond to individual sounds (phonemes).
2. **Three subtypes** -- pure alphabets (Greek, Latin), abjads (Arabic, Hebrew), abugidas (Devanagari, Tibetan).
3. **Compact symbol set** -- typically fewer than 100 distinct symbols.
4. **Proto-Sinaitic origin** -- most alphabets descend from or were inspired by the Proto-Sinaitic script (c. 1800 BC).
5. **Latin dominance** -- the Latin alphabet is by far the most common script used by writing systems worldwide.

## Context & Application

Abjads were the first alphabets to develop historically, used for Semitic languages where consonantal notation is sufficient due to morphological patterns. The Greek alphabet (c. 800 BC) added dedicated vowel letters, creating the first pure alphabet. The Brahmic family of abugidas is the largest single group, covering nearly all scripts used in India and Southeast Asia ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/)): The Latin alphabet descended from the Greek alphabet, which descended from the Phoenician alphabet, which descended from the Proto-Sinaitic script.

**Example 2** ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/)): English orthography uses 26 Latin letters with extensive polyvalence (deep orthography), while Finnish uses Latin letters with more consistent sound-letter mappings (shallow orthography).
