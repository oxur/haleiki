## Quick Definition

Earth's inner core is a solid ball of primarily iron-nickel alloy with a radius of about 1,220 km, at temperatures comparable to the surface of the Sun. It remains solid despite extreme heat because of the immense pressure at Earth's center.

## Core Definition

Earth's inner core is the innermost geologic layer of the planet, primarily a solid ball with a radius of about 1,230 km, about 20% of Earth's radius ([earths-inner-core](/source/earths-inner-core/)). It is believed to be composed of an iron-nickel alloy with some lighter elements. The temperature at its surface is estimated to be approximately 5,700 K. It is solid at high temperature because of its high pressure, in accordance with the Simon-Glatzel equation ([earths-inner-core](/source/earths-inner-core/)). The inner core was discovered in 1936 by Inge Lehmann through the study of seismograms.

## Prerequisites

- **Earth's outer core** — The inner core is surrounded by the liquid outer core; understanding this relationship is essential

## Key Properties

1. Radius of approximately 1,221 km (about 19% of Earth's radius)
2. Composed primarily of iron-nickel alloy, likely in hexagonal close-packed (epsilon-iron) crystal form
3. Surface temperature approximately 5,700 K (comparable to the Sun's surface)
4. Pressure ranges from about 330 to 360 GPa
5. Density of about 12.8-13.0 kg/L
6. Exhibits seismic anisotropy: P waves travel about 1% faster in the north-south direction
7. May contain an "innermost inner core" with different properties
8. Age estimated between 0.5 and 2 billion years

## Context & Application

The inner core's growth from the cooling outer core provides the compositional buoyancy that drives the geodynamo and generates Earth's magnetic field. Its crystallization releases light elements into the outer core, creating convection currents ([earths-inner-core](/source/earths-inner-core/)). Recent research (2023) reported that the inner core's rotation relative to the mantle may oscillate with a period of about seven decades.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([earths-inner-core](/source/earths-inner-core/)): In 1936, Inge Lehmann deduced the existence of a solid inner core from seismic wave reflections, initially estimating its radius at 1,400 km.

**Example 2** ([earths-inner-core](/source/earths-inner-core/)): Laboratory experiments at core-like pressures using diamond anvil cells strongly supported the theory that the inner core is made of giant crystals running north to south.

## Relationships

- **Earth's outer core** surrounds the inner core and is fluid, contrasting with the inner core's solid state
- **Geodynamo** is sustained partly by the growth of the inner core and release of light elements into the outer core

## Common Confusions

- **The inner core is not molten**: Despite its extreme temperature, it remains solid due to the immense pressure
- **Inner core rotation**: The inner core does not spin independently; it may rotate slightly faster or slower than the mantle, oscillating with a ~70-year period
