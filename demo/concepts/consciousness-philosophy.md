## Quick Definition

Consciousness, in philosophy of mind, refers to the subjective quality of experience and awareness. It encompasses both "access consciousness" (aspects of experience that can be apprehended and reported) and "phenomenal consciousness" (the qualitative "what it is like" character of experience).

## Core Definition

Philosophy of mind deals with the nature of the mind, where consciousness and its neural correlates are central aspects of study ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Descartes was the first to clearly identify the mind with consciousness and self-awareness. In the philosophical literature, perhaps the most common taxonomy of consciousness is into "access" and "phenomenal" variants: access consciousness concerns those aspects of experience that can be apprehended, while phenomenal consciousness concerns those aspects that are characterized qualitatively in terms of "raw feels," "what it is like," or qualia ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)). Cognitive science examines consciousness alongside perception, memory, attention, reasoning, language, and emotion ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)).

## Key Properties

1. Two main types: access consciousness (reportable content) and phenomenal consciousness (qualitative experience)
2. Central to the mind-body problem and the "hard problem"
3. Has neural correlates (NCC) studied by neuroscience
4. Descartes first systematically identified mind with consciousness
5. Whether non-biological systems can be conscious is a major open question

## Context & Application

Consciousness is the focus of the hard problem of consciousness (why subjective experience arises from physical processes) and is central to debates about artificial intelligence and machine sentience. Colin McGinn argues humans may be "cognitively closed" to understanding their own consciousness, while Thomas Nagel suggests a future paradigm shift may be needed ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)): David Chalmers argues LLMs today display impressive intelligence but likely lack consciousness, as they lack recurrent processing, a global workspace, and unified agency.

**Example 2** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): Thomas Nagel's famous question "What is it like to be a bat?" highlights the difficulty of understanding consciousness from a third-person perspective.

## Relationships

- **Mind-body problem** asks how consciousness relates to physical processes
- **Qualia** are the qualitative aspects of conscious experience
- **Hard problem of consciousness** specifically targets the explanation of phenomenal consciousness
- **Artificial consciousness** asks whether machines can be conscious
- **Cognitive science** studies consciousness empirically
