## Quick Definition

Sunyata (emptiness) is the Buddhist doctrine that all phenomena are empty of inherent existence (svabhava) because they arise in dependence on causes, conditions, and conceptual designation. Emptiness itself is also empty -- it does not refer to a transcendental reality.

## Core Definition

Sunyata refers to the idea that all phenomena (dharmas) are empty (sunya) of "nature," of any "substance" or "essence" (svabhava) which could give them "solid and independent existence," because they are dependently co-arisen. But this "emptiness" itself is also "empty": it does not have an existence on its own, nor does it refer to a transcendental reality beyond or above phenomenal reality ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)). The importance of the theory of emptiness is central to the Tantric Buddhist view and practice. The Buddhist emptiness view sees the world as fluid, without an ontological foundation or inherent existence ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Absence of svabhava** -- phenomena lack inherent existence, substance, or essence ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
2. **Emptiness of emptiness** -- emptiness itself is empty; it is not a transcendental reality ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
3. **Central to Vajrayana** -- emptiness is foundational to tantric practice; self-visualization as the deity is no less real than everyday reality because both lack inherent existence ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).
4. **Equated with dependent arising** -- Nagarjuna states "conditioned origination is emptiness" ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
