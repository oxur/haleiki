## Quick Definition

A musical canon is a contrapuntal compositional technique and form in which a melody is imitated exactly by one or more voices entering at successive time intervals. It is both a compositional technique and a musical form.

## Core Definition

A musical canon is both a compositional technique and a musical form in which one melody is imitated exactly by other voices entering at fixed time intervals. Encyclopaedia Britannica calls a "canon" both a compositional technique and a musical form ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). In medieval Italy, polyphonic canons (songs in which multiple singers sing the same melody, but starting at different times) were called caccie, featuring two higher voices in canon with an underlying instrumental long-note accompaniment ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). Johann Sebastian Bach used canon extensively in works such as The Musical Offering and the Goldberg Variations ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)).

## Prerequisites

- **counterpoint** -- Canon is a strict form of contrapuntal writing requiring understanding of voice interaction

## Key Properties

1. A melody is imitated exactly by one or more voices
2. Voices enter at successive time intervals (the time interval is called the "period")
3. The leading voice is the "dux" (leader); following voices are "comes" (followers)
4. Canons can operate at different intervals (unison, fifth, octave, etc.)
5. Special types include retrograde canons, inversion canons, and augmentation/diminution canons
6. The "round" (e.g., "Row, Row, Row Your Boat") is the simplest type of canon

## Construction / Recognition

1. Compose or select a melody (the dux)
2. Set a time interval for the second voice to enter
3. The second voice (comes) exactly imitates the first voice's melody
4. Ensure that vertical sonorities between voices produce acceptable harmonies
5. Optionally add free (non-canonic) voices for harmonic support
6. The canon may end with a coda that breaks the strict imitation

## Context & Application

The canon has been used from the medieval period through the present day. Medieval Italian caccie were among the earliest canonic forms ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). Bach brought the canon to extraordinary sophistication, particularly in the Goldberg Variations and The Musical Offering, where he explored every conceivable type of canonic technique ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)).

## Examples

- **polyphony** -- Canon produces a polyphonic texture through its imitative entries
- **counterpoint** -- Canon is a specific, strict application of contrapuntal technique
