## Quick Definition

General relativity is the geometric theory of gravitation published by Albert Einstein in 1916. It describes gravity not as a force but as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy, summarized as "spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve."

## Core Definition

General relativity is the geometric theory of gravitation that generalizes special relativity and refines Newton's law of universal gravitation, providing a unified description of gravity as a geometric property of four-dimensional spacetime. The curvature of spacetime is directly related to the energy, momentum, and stress of whatever matter and radiation is present, as specified by the Einstein field equations: $G_{\mu\nu} \equiv R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}R\,g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)). John Archibald Wheeler summarized it: "Space-time tells matter how to move; matter tells space-time how to curve" ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Special relativity** -- general relativity generalizes special relativity to include gravity; the equivalence principle and Lorentz invariance are essential prerequisites.

## Key Properties

1. **Gravity as geometry** -- gravitational phenomena arise from spacetime curvature, not a force acting at a distance ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
2. **Einstein field equations** -- ten coupled nonlinear partial differential equations relating the geometry of spacetime to the distribution of matter-energy ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
3. **Geodesic motion** -- freely falling particles follow geodesics in curved spacetime ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
4. **General covariance** -- the laws take the same form in all coordinate systems, with no preferred background geometry ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
5. **Reduces to Newton** -- in the weak-field, low-speed limit, general relativity reproduces Newtonian gravity ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
6. **Predictions beyond Newton** -- gravitational time dilation, gravitational lensing, gravitational redshift, gravitational waves, black holes, and Big Bang cosmology ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Context & Application

Einstein developed general relativity between 1907 and 1915, building on Riemannian geometry as suggested by Marcel Grossmann. The 1919 Eddington expedition confirmed the predicted deflection of starlight by the Sun. General relativity provides the framework for modern cosmology (Big Bang, cosmic expansion, dark energy) and astrophysics (black holes, neutron stars, gravitational waves). LIGO's 2016 direct detection of gravitational waves was a landmark confirmation ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)). Reconciling general relativity with quantum mechanics remains one of the deepest open problems in physics ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): The anomalous perihelion precession of Mercury, unexplained by Newtonian gravity, is naturally predicted by general relativity.

**Example 2** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): Gravitational time dilation, confirmed by atomic clocks and essential for GPS satellite corrections, where clocks in stronger gravitational fields run slower: $t_0 = t_f \sqrt{1 - \frac{2GM}{rc^2}}$.

**Example 3** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): The Schwarzschild solution describes the geometry around a non-rotating spherical mass and predicts the existence of black holes.

## Relationships

- **Extends special-relativity**: general relativity incorporates gravity into the special-relativistic framework.
- **Extends classical-mechanics**: Newtonian gravity is the weak-field limit of general relativity.
