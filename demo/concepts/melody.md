## Quick Definition

A melody is a group of musical sounds in succession -- a tune or linear arrangement of pitches. It is often the most prominent aspect of music, and its construction and qualities are a primary interest of music theory.

## Core Definition

A melody is a group of sounds in succession, a tune, or an arrangement. Melody is often a prominent aspect of music, and so its construction and qualities are a primary interest of music theory ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The basic elements of melody are pitch, duration, rhythm, and tempo. The tones of a melody are usually drawn from pitch systems such as scales or modes. Melody may consist, to increasing degree, of the figure, motive, semi-phrase, antecedent and consequent phrase, and period or sentence ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Key Properties

1. Melody is the horizontal (successive) dimension of music
2. Its basic elements are pitch, duration, rhythm, and tempo
3. Melodic tones are typically drawn from scales or modes
4. Melodic structure ranges from small figures and motives to complete periods
5. A period may be considered the complete melody, though some examples combine two periods

## Context & Application

Melody is central to virtually all musical traditions worldwide. In Western music theory, the relationship between melody and harmony forms the core of compositional practice. In the medieval period, music was primarily monophonic (single melody without accompaniment), while the Renaissance and later periods increasingly explored how melodies interact through counterpoint and polyphony ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)).

## Relationships

- **harmony** -- Melody is the horizontal complement to harmony's vertical dimension; together they define the basic fabric of Western music
- **rhythm** -- Rhythm is one of the basic elements of melody, providing temporal structure to the pitch sequence
- **scale-music** -- Scales provide the pitch material from which melodies are typically constructed
- **mode-music** -- Modes, like scales, provide organizational frameworks for melodic construction
