## Quick Definition

The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence was a 1956 summer workshop at Dartmouth College, widely considered the founding event of artificial intelligence as a field, where John McCarthy coined the term "artificial intelligence."

## Core Definition

The Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence was a 1956 summer workshop widely considered to be the founding event of artificial intelligence as a field, referred to as "the Constitutional Convention of AI" ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)). The project's four organizers -- Claude Shannon, John McCarthy, Nathaniel Rochester, and Marvin Minsky -- are considered some of the founding fathers of AI ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)). The project lasted approximately six to eight weeks and was essentially an extended brainstorming session ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)). McCarthy chose the name "artificial intelligence" partly for its neutrality, avoiding narrow automata theory and cybernetics ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Founding event** -- widely considered the founding event of AI as an academic discipline ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).
2. **Coined the term AI** -- the 1955 proposal is credited with introducing the term "artificial intelligence" ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).
3. **Key conjecture** -- "every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it" ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).
4. **Scope** -- discussed computers, natural language processing, neural networks, theory of computation, abstraction, and creativity -- areas still relevant to the field ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).
5. **Funded by Rockefeller Foundation** -- McCarthy approached the foundation in 1955 for funding ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).

## Context & Application

In the early 1950s, there were various names for the field of "thinking machines": cybernetics, automata theory, and complex information processing. McCarthy decided to organize a group to clarify and develop ideas about thinking machines and picked the name "artificial intelligence" for the new field ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)). The proposal was formally made on September 2, 1955, by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester, and Shannon ([dartmouth-workshop](/source/dartmouth-workshop/)).
