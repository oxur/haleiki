## Quick Definition

Padmasambhava ("Born from a Lotus"), also known as Guru Rinpoche ("Precious Guru"), was a semi-legendary tantric Buddhist Vajra master from medieval India who is credited with fully revealing the Vajrayana in Tibet and founding the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism.

## Core Definition

Padmasambhava was a semi-legendary tantric Buddhist Vajra master from medieval India, who according to hagiographical sources fully revealed the Vajrayana in Tibet, circa 8th-9th centuries. He is considered an emanation of Shakyamuni Buddha. According to early Tibetan sources including the Testament of Ba, he came to Tibet in the 8th century and designed Samye Monastery, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet, during the reign of King Trisong Detsen. He, the king, and Khenpo Shantarakshita are also responsible for creating the Tibetan canon through translating all of the Buddha's teachings into Tibetan ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). In modern Tibetan Buddhism, Padmasambhava is considered to be a Buddha. The Nyingma school considers Padmasambhava to be a founding figure ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Founding figure** -- credited with founding the Nyingma school and establishing Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet.
2. **Historical authenticity** -- while questioned by earlier scholars, his historical existence is now "cautiously accepted" by modern Tibetologists ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
3. **Eight manifestations** -- Padmasambhava is represented through eight principal forms (Guru Tsen Gye), each manifesting different aspects (wrathful, peaceful) at different points in his life ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
4. **Terma tradition** -- Padmasambhava concealed numerous hidden treasure texts (terma) for discovery by future tertöns when conditions were ripe ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
5. **Two lineages** -- his teachings include an oral lineage (kama) and a lineage of hidden treasure texts (terma) ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).
6. **Cross-school significance** -- tantric cycles related to Padmasambhava are practiced not only by the Nyingma but also by figures from the Kagyu, Sakya, and Gelug schools ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Context & Application

According to the Testament of Ba, Shantarakshita invited Padmasambhava to Tibet to tame local spirits and impress Tibetans with his ritual powers. After subduing the spirits, the construction of Samye proceeded. Padmasambhava also taught various forms of tantric Buddhist yoga ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Starting from the 12th century, extensive hagiographies expanded his profile, with Nyangral Nyima Ozer's Copper Palace becoming the first full biography. The Padmasambhava narrative grew to dominate other founding narratives by the end of the 12th century ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Padmasambhava is widely venerated across Tibet, Nepal, Bhutan, and the Himalayan regions. The Paro Taktsang ("Tiger's Nest") monastery in Bhutan, built on a sheer cliff, is one of the most famous sites associated with him.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): The Vajra Guru mantra -- "Om ah hum vajra guru padma siddhi hum" -- is one of the most widely recited mantras in Tibetan Buddhism, associated with communion with Padmasambhava's Three Vajras.

**Example 2** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): Among Padmasambhava's eight manifestations, Guru Pema Jungne ("Born from a Lotus") is the most commonly depicted, representing the Vajrayana Buddha who teaches the Dharma to the people.

**Example 3** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): The Seven Line Prayer to Padmasambhava is a well-known daily prayer said to contain the most sacred teachings of Dzogchen.

## Relationships

- **Related to nyingma** -- Padmasambhava is the founding figure of the Nyingma school.
- **Related to vajrayana** -- Padmasambhava is credited with fully revealing the Vajrayana in Tibet.
- **Related to dzogchen** -- the Nyingma's Dzogchen lineage is transmitted through Padmasambhava from Garab Dorje.
- **Related to terma** -- Padmasambhava concealed hidden treasure texts throughout Tibet for later discovery.
