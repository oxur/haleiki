## Quick Definition

Madhyamaka ("middle way") is a tradition of Buddhist philosophy founded by Nagarjuna (c. 150-250 CE) that argues all phenomena are empty (sunya) of inherent existence (svabhava) because they are dependently co-arisen. It is the dominant philosophical interpretation in Tibetan Buddhism.

## Core Definition

Madhyamaka refers to a tradition of Buddhist philosophy and practice founded by the Indian Buddhist philosopher Nagarjuna. The foundational text is Nagarjuna's Mulamadhyamakakarika ("Root Verses on the Middle Way") ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)). According to classical Indian Madhyamaka thinkers, all phenomena (dharmas) are empty (sunya) of "nature," of any "substance" or "essence" (svabhava) which could give them "solid and independent existence," because they are dependently co-arisen. But this "emptiness" itself is also "empty" -- it does not have an existence on its own, nor does it refer to a transcendental reality beyond phenomenal reality ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)). Madhyamaka discerns two levels of truth: conventional truth (everyday commonsense reality) and ultimate truth (emptiness) ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)). The philosophical view of Vajrayana is based on Mahayana Buddhist philosophy, mainly the Madhyamaka and Yogacara schools ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Emptiness of svabhava** -- all phenomena are empty of inherent existence, substance, or essence; nothing exists independently of causes, conditions, and concepts ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
2. **Emptiness of emptiness** -- emptiness itself is empty; it does not refer to a transcendental reality or have existence on its own ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
3. **Two truths** -- conventional truth (samvrti satya, the everyday world) and ultimate truth (paramartha satya, emptiness); the distinction is primarily epistemological, not ontological ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
4. **Dependent arising** -- Nagarjuna's key argument: "conditioned origination is emptiness"; whatever is dependently arisen is unceasing, unborn, and free from conceptual construction ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
5. **Prasanga method** -- Madhyamaka uses reductio ad absurdum arguments (prasanga) to demonstrate that essentialist ideas lead to contradictions ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).
6. **Cognitive shift required** -- understanding Madhyamaka reasoning is not sufficient; a cognitive shift (realization) through practice is needed to end the suffering caused by reification ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)).

## Context & Application

Since the 4th century CE, Madhyamaka philosophy had a major influence on the subsequent development of Mahayana Buddhism, especially following the spread of Buddhism throughout Asia. It is the dominant interpretation of Buddhist philosophy in Tibetan Buddhism ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)). In the Nyingma tradition, Rongzom Chokyi Zangpo held that sutra teachings such as Madhyamaka were ultimately inferior to the teachings found in the Buddhist Tantras and Dzogchen ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Mipham the Great attempted to harmonize the view of Dzogchen with Madhyamaka ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Jamgon Kongtrul promoted "Shentong Madhyamaka" as a valid form that holds the ultimate truth is nondual, self-aware primordial wisdom ([jamgon-kongtrul](/source/jamgon-kongtrul/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)): Nagarjuna's Diamond Splinters argument demonstrates that nothing truly arises: a thing cannot arise from itself (would be meaningless), from something other (anything could arise from anything), from both, or without any cause.

**Example 2** ([madhyamaka](/source/madhyamaka/)): Nagarjuna's dedicatory verse: "I prostrate to the Perfect Buddha, the best of teachers, who taught that whatever is dependently arisen is unceasing, unborn, unannihilated, not permanent, not coming, not going, without distinction, without identity, and free from conceptual construction."

## Relationships

- **Related to vajrayana** -- Madhyamaka provides the philosophical foundation for Vajrayana practice.
- **Related to nyingma** -- the Nyingma school engages extensively with Madhyamaka, with varying views on its relationship to Dzogchen.
- **Related to sunyata** -- sunyata (emptiness) is the central concept of Madhyamaka.
- **Related to two-truths** -- the two truths doctrine structures Madhyamaka's account of reality.
- **Related to dependent-arising** -- dependent arising is equated with emptiness in Madhyamaka.
