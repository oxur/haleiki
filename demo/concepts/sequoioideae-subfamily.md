## Quick Definition

Sequoioideae is a subfamily of coniferous trees within the family Cupressaceae, commonly known as redwoods. It includes the largest and tallest trees in the world across three living genera: Sequoia (coast redwood), Sequoiadendron (giant sequoia), and Metasequoia (dawn redwood).

## Core Definition

Sequoioideae, commonly referred to as redwoods, is a subfamily of coniferous trees within the family Cupressaceae that range in the northern hemisphere. It includes the largest and tallest trees in the world ([sequoioideae](/source/sequoioideae/)). The three extant genera are Sequoia from coastal California and Oregon, Sequoiadendron from California's Sierra Nevada, and Metasequoia in China. The trees can live for thousands of years. The subfamily is ancient, with the oldest described species (Sequoia jeholensis) from Jurassic deposits. The entire subfamily is endangered ([sequoioideae](/source/sequoioideae/)).

## Key Properties

1. Subfamily of Cupressaceae (cypress family), order Cupressales
2. Three living genera: Sequoia, Sequoiadendron, and Metasequoia
3. Includes the largest and tallest trees in the world
4. Gymnosperm (conifer), not an angiosperm
5. Ancient lineage: earliest fossils from the Jurassic period
6. Reached peak diversity during the early Cenozoic
7. All three extant species are IUCN Endangered
8. Evidence for reticulate evolution: Sequoia may be a hybrid of Metasequoia and Sequoiadendron ancestors

## Context & Application

The redwood subfamily illustrates remarkable evolutionary longevity -- fossils date to the Jurassic -- combined with current vulnerability. Once widespread across the northern hemisphere, the subfamily has contracted to three relict species due to climate cooling and ice ages. In 2024, about one-fifth of all giant sequoias were destroyed in extreme California wildfires over two years ([sequoioideae](/source/sequoioideae/)).
