## Quick Definition

Spacetime is the four-dimensional mathematical model that fuses three spatial dimensions with one time dimension into a single continuum. In general relativity, the geometry of spacetime is dynamic and curves in response to mass-energy.

## Core Definition

General relativity provides a unified description of gravity as a geometric property of space and time, or four-dimensional spacetime. The curvature of spacetime is directly related to the energy, momentum, and stress of whatever matter and radiation is present ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)). In special relativity, spacetime is modeled as flat Minkowski space; in general relativity, it is a curved pseudo-Riemannian manifold whose geometry is determined by the Einstein field equations ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Four-dimensional** -- three spatial dimensions plus one time dimension ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
2. **Dynamic geometry** -- in general relativity, the geometry of spacetime is not fixed but responds to the distribution of matter-energy ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
3. **Causal structure** -- light cones define which events can influence others ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
4. **Metric tensor** -- the metric describes how distances and angles are measured in spacetime ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
5. **Locally Minkowskian** -- at small scales, spacetime always looks like flat Minkowski space (the equivalence principle) ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Context & Application

The concept of spacetime originated with Hermann Minkowski's 1908 geometric interpretation of special relativity. Einstein's general relativity (1915) made spacetime dynamic: mass-energy curves spacetime, and curved spacetime dictates the motion of matter. Spacetime is the arena for all of modern cosmology and gravitational physics ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): The Schwarzschild metric describes spacetime around a non-rotating spherical mass, predicting phenomena such as gravitational time dilation and black holes.

**Example 2** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): The Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker metric describes the spacetime of an expanding universe, providing the framework for Big Bang cosmology.
