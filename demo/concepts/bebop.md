## Quick Definition

Bebop is a style of jazz that emerged in the 1940s, characterized by fast tempos, complex harmonies, virtuosic improvisation, and a shift from danceable popular music toward a more challenging "musician's music."

## Core Definition

Bebop emerged in the 1940s, shifting jazz from danceable popular music toward a more challenging "musician's music" which was played at faster tempos and used more chord-based improvisation ([jazz](/source/jazz/)). Cool jazz emerged as a reaction to bop, characterized by more moderate tempos and "a more reflective attitude" ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)). Bebop is one of the stylistic origins of cool jazz, alongside swing and classical elements ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)).

## Prerequisites

- **jazz** -- Bebop is a subgenre of jazz, requiring understanding of the jazz tradition

## Key Properties

1. Emerged in the 1940s
2. Fast tempos and complex harmonies
3. Chord-based improvisation rather than melody-based
4. Shifted jazz from popular entertainment to "musician's music"
5. Key figures include Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie
6. Provoked the cool jazz reaction with its intensity and complexity

## Context & Application

Bebop represented a revolutionary break in jazz, transforming it from entertainment music to an art form demanding high technical virtuosity. The style's intensity and complexity provoked the calmer, more restrained cool jazz reaction in the late 1940s ([jazz](/source/jazz/), [cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)): Miles Davis's solo on Charlie Parker's "Chasin' the Bird" in 1947 anticipated the Cool Era, showing how bebop musicians were already exploring calmer alternatives.

**Example 2** ([jazz](/source/jazz/)): Bebop shifted jazz from the danceable swing big bands of the 1930s to smaller ensembles playing at faster tempos with more complex harmonies.
