## Quick Definition

Eucalyptus is a genus of over 700 species of flowering plants in the family Myrtaceae, mostly native to Australia. It includes the tallest known flowering plant on Earth (Eucalyptus regnans) and species of major economic importance for timber, pulpwood, and essential oils.

## Core Definition

Eucalyptus is a genus of more than 700 species of flowering plants in the family Myrtaceae. Most species are trees, often mallees, and a few are shrubs. Along with other genera in the tribe Eucalypteae, they are commonly known as eucalypts or "gum trees" ([eucalyptus](/source/eucalyptus/)). Species have bark that is either smooth, fibrous, hard, or stringy and leaves with oil glands. The sepals and petals are fused to form a "cap" or operculum over the stamens, hence the name from Greek "well covered" ([eucalyptus](/source/eucalyptus/)). The largest angiosperms are Eucalyptus gum trees of Australia, which can reach almost 100 m in height ([flowering-plant](/source/flowering-plant/)).

## Key Properties

1. Over 700 species in the family Myrtaceae
2. Mostly native to Australia; present in every state and territory
3. Includes the tallest known flowering plant (E. regnans)
4. Characteristic fused sepals/petals forming an operculum
5. Leaves contain oil glands
6. Fast-growing; valuable for timber, pulpwood, honey, and essential oils
7. High flammability poses forest fire danger
8. Forms ectomycorrhizal associations

## Context & Application

Eucalyptus trees have been planted globally for their fast growth and economic value. However, their high flammability has led to removal in some countries. E. globulus bark can photosynthesize in the absence of foliage, allowing survival after fire damage ([eucalyptus](/source/eucalyptus/)).
