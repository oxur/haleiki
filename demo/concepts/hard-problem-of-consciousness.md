## Quick Definition

The hard problem of consciousness, formulated by David Chalmers, asks why and how subjective qualitative experience (qualia) arises from physical brain processes. It is distinguished from "easy problems" of consciousness that concern functional and behavioral explanations of cognitive processes.

## Core Definition

Explaining why and how subjective experience arises is known as the hard problem of consciousness ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)). The puzzle is why many cerebral processes occur with an accompanying experiential aspect in consciousness that seems impossible to explain by reference to the physical properties of the brain alone ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). If an attempt to explain a phenomenon reductively succeeds, it should explain why the phenomenon has all of its properties; in the case of mental states, this means explaining why they have the property of being experienced in a certain way ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Consciousness** — The hard problem is specifically about phenomenal consciousness
- **Qualia** — The subjective qualities whose existence generates the hard problem

## Key Properties

1. Asks "why" subjective experience exists, not merely "how" brain processes correlate with it
2. Distinguishes from "easy problems" (explaining cognitive functions, behavior, neural correlates)
3. The explanatory gap: no amount of physical/functional description seems to fully explain subjective experience
4. Central to debates about artificial consciousness
5. Some philosophers (mysterianism) argue it may be permanently unsolvable for human minds

## Context & Application

The hard problem is particularly relevant to artificial consciousness research: even if we build a system that functionally duplicates a brain, it remains unclear whether it would have subjective experience ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)). Colin McGinn argues humans may be "cognitively closed" regarding the hard problem, while Thomas Nagel suggests a future paradigm shift may be needed ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): One can know all the objective information about seeing red (brain states, wavelengths of light), yet still not know what it is like to see red.

## Relationships

- **Consciousness** is the phenomenon the hard problem concerns
- **Qualia** are the specific aspects of experience that generate the problem
- **Mind-body problem** is the broader context within which the hard problem sits
- **Artificial consciousness** research must contend with the hard problem
