## Quick Definition

Perception is the organization, identification, and interpretation of sensory information to represent and understand the environment. It is not merely passive reception of stimuli but is actively shaped by learning, memory, expectation, and attention.

## Core Definition

Perception is the organization, identification, and interpretation of sensory information, in order to represent and understand the presented information or environment ([perception](/source/perception/)). All perception involves signals that go through the nervous system resulting from physical or chemical stimulation of the sensory system. Perception is not only the passive receipt of these signals but is also shaped by the recipient's learning, memory, expectation, and attention ([perception](/source/perception/)). The process transforms low-level sensory information to higher-level information (e.g., extracting shapes for object recognition). Cognitive science includes perception as one of the central mental faculties it studies ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Cognitive science** — Perception is a central topic within cognitive science

## Key Properties

1. Involves organization, identification, and interpretation of sensory information
2. Actively shaped by learning, memory, expectation, and attention (not merely passive)
3. Types include vision, hearing, touch, taste, smell, and proprioception
4. The brain's perceptual systems enable stable experience despite incomplete, rapidly varying sensory input
5. Multistable perception: ambiguous stimuli can be perceived in multiple ways (e.g., Necker cube, Rubin vase)
6. Processing occurs largely outside conscious awareness

## Context & Application

Perceptual issues in philosophy include the extent to which sensory qualities such as sound, smell, or color exist in objective reality rather than in the mind of the perceiver ([perception](/source/perception/)). This connects perception to the philosophy of mind debates about qualia and the nature of subjective experience. Jerome Bruner's model describes perception as a three-stage process from openness to cues, through categorization, to selective confirmation.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([perception](/source/perception/)): The Necker cube and Rubin vase demonstrate multistable perception: a single stimulus can be perceived in more than one way.

**Example 2** ([perception](/source/perception/)): In vision, the timing from photoreceptor activation to EEG detection of an anomalous word takes about 230 milliseconds.

## Relationships

- **Eye** is the primary sensory organ for visual perception
- **Consciousness** intersects with perception through the question of what makes perceptual experience subjective
- **Qualia** are the subjective qualities of perceptual experiences
