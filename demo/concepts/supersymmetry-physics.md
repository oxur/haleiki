## Quick Definition

Supersymmetry is a theoretical symmetry between bosons (integer-spin force carriers) and fermions (half-integer-spin matter particles), proposing that every known particle has a yet-undiscovered superpartner with spin differing by one-half.

## Core Definition

Supersymmetry is a theoretical framework in physics that suggests a symmetry between particles with integer spin (bosons) and particles with half-integer spin (fermions). It proposes that for every known particle, there exists a partner particle with different spin properties. In theories with supersymmetry, each boson has a counterpart which is a fermion, and vice versa ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)). When supersymmetry is imposed as a local symmetry, Einstein's theory of general relativity is included automatically, yielding supergravity ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Quantum mechanics** -- supersymmetry extends the symmetry structure of quantum field theory; understanding quantum states and spin is essential.

## Key Properties

1. **Boson-fermion pairing** -- every boson has a fermionic superpartner and vice versa ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).
2. **Spin difference of 1/2** -- superpartners differ in spin by exactly one-half ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).
3. **Extends Poincare symmetry** -- supersymmetry is the only known way to extend spacetime symmetry beyond the Poincare group (Haag-Lopuszanski-Sohnius theorem) ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).
4. **Implies supergravity** -- local supersymmetry automatically includes gravity ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).
5. **Not yet observed** -- no superpartners have been detected at current experimental energies ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).

## Context & Application

Supersymmetry was first proposed in the context of string theory by Ramond, Schwarz, and Neveu (1971), and developed for quantum field theory by Wess and Zumino (1974). It plays a central role in superstring theory, M-theory, and supergravity. The Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM) was proposed in 1977 as a solution to the hierarchy problem. Despite extensive searches at the LHC, no experimental evidence has been found ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([supersymmetry](/source/supersymmetry/)): If the electron existed in a supersymmetric theory, there would be a bosonic partner called the "selectron" (superpartner electron).

**Example 2** ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)): In M-theory, supersymmetry restricts spacetime to at most eleven dimensions and determines the spectrum of branes that can exist.

- **Related to superstring-theory**: superstring theory incorporates supersymmetry, pairing bosonic and fermionic string modes.
- **Related to supergravity**: local supersymmetry yields supergravity theories.
- **Related to m-theory-physics**: M-theory unifies all supersymmetric string theories.
