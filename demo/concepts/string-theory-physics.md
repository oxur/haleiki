## Quick Definition

String theory is a theoretical framework in which the point-like particles of particle physics are replaced by one-dimensional objects called strings, whose different vibrational states correspond to different particles. It naturally incorporates gravity, making it a candidate theory of quantum gravity.

## Core Definition

In physics, string theory is a theoretical framework in which the point-like particles of particle physics are replaced by one-dimensional objects called strings. On distance scales larger than the string scale, a string acts like a particle, with its mass, charge, and other properties determined by the vibrational state of the string. One of the vibrational states corresponds to the graviton, making string theory a theory of quantum gravity ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)). String theory requires extra dimensions of spacetime for mathematical consistency: 26 dimensions for bosonic string theory, 10 for superstring theory, and 11 for M-theory ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Quantum mechanics** -- string theory is a quantum theory; the vibrational states of strings are quantum mechanical.
- **General relativity** -- string theory aims to reconcile gravity with quantum mechanics; understanding GR is essential context.

## Key Properties

1. **Extended objects** -- particles are modeled as one-dimensional strings rather than zero-dimensional points ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).
2. **Vibrational spectrum** -- different vibrational modes correspond to different particles; one mode gives the graviton ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).
3. **Extra dimensions** -- mathematical consistency requires 10 or 11 spacetime dimensions ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).
4. **Dualities** -- S-duality and T-duality relate seemingly different string theories to each other ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).
5. **Five consistent versions** -- type I, type IIA, type IIB, and two heterotic theories, all unified by M-theory ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).
6. **Not yet experimentally tested** -- the Planck-scale ($10^{-35}$ m) string length is far beyond current experimental reach ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).

## Context & Application

String theory originated in the late 1960s as a theory of the strong force (Veneziano amplitude), but was abandoned in favor of QCD. In 1974, Yoneya, Schwarz, and Scherk recognized it as a theory of gravity. The first superstring revolution (1984) established its viability. Witten's 1995 M-theory conjecture unified the five versions. The 1997 AdS/CFT correspondence connected string theory to quantum field theory. Applications extend to black hole entropy, nuclear physics (quark-gluon plasma), and pure mathematics (mirror symmetry, monstrous moonshine) ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)).
