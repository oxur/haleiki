## Quick Definition

Homophony is a musical texture in which a single melodic line is accompanied by chords. It contrasts with polyphony (multiple independent melodies) and monophony (a single unaccompanied melody).

## Core Definition

Homophonic texture consists of chords accompanying a melody ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). In homophony, one voice carries the primary melodic line while other voices or instruments provide harmonic support through chords or chord-like patterns. This contrasts with polyphonic texture, where multiple independent melodic lines sound simultaneously, and monophonic texture, where a single melody stands alone.

## Key Properties

1. Features a single prominent melody supported by chordal accompaniment
2. The accompanying voices move together rhythmically (homorhythmic) or provide arpeggiated support
3. Contrasts with polyphony (independent voices) and monophony (single voice)
4. Became increasingly dominant in Western music from the Baroque period onward
5. Standard texture for much popular, folk, and Classical-era music

## Context & Application

Homophonic texture became increasingly common as Western music moved from the Renaissance emphasis on polyphony toward the Baroque and Classical emphasis on melody with harmonic accompaniment. Most popular music, hymns, and Classical-era instrumental music uses homophonic texture ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Relationships

- **harmony** -- Homophony is the texture that most directly expresses harmonic relationships
- **chord** -- Chords provide the accompaniment in homophonic texture
- **polyphony** -- Polyphony contrasts with homophony by featuring independent melodic lines
- **monophony** -- Monophony contrasts with homophony by having no accompaniment at all
