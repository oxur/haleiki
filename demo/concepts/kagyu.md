## Quick Definition

The Kagyu ("Oral Lineage") school is one of the four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism, tracing its lineage from the 11th-century Indian mahasiddhas Naropa and Maitripa through Marpa Lotsawa to Tibet. Its principal teachings are Mahamudra and the Six Dharmas of Naropa.

## Core Definition

The Kagyu school translates to "Oral Lineage" or "Whispered Transmission" school and is one of the four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism. The Kagyu lineages trace themselves back to the 11th-century Indian mahasiddhas Naropa, Maitripa, and the yogini Niguma, via their student Marpa Lotsawa (1012-1097), who brought their teachings to Tibet ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)). Marpa's student Milarepa was also an influential poet and teacher. The principal Kagyu lineages existing today stem from Milarepa's disciple Gampopa (1079-1153), who merged the Kagyu lineage with the Kadam tradition. The main teachings include Mahamudra and the Six Dharmas of Naropa ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Vajrayana** -- the Kagyu school is a Vajrayana Buddhist tradition; understanding the tantric framework is necessary context.

## Key Properties

1. **Oral transmission lineage** -- the name "Kagyu" (bka' brgyud) refers to the oral transmission of esoteric teaching from teacher to disciple ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).
2. **Indian origins** -- traces through Tilopa, Naropa, and Maitripa in India before Marpa brought teachings to Tibet ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).
3. **Mahamudra emphasis** -- the "close lineage" of Mahamudra from Tilopa through Naropa to Marpa, and the "distant lineage" through Saraha and Maitripa ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).
4. **Six Dharmas of Naropa** -- completion stage practices of highest yoga tantras using subtle body energetics (energy-winds, channels, drops) ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).
5. **Multiple sub-schools** -- Karma Kagyu (largest, headed by the Karmapa), Drikung Kagyu, Drukpa Lineage, Taklung Kagyu, and Shangpa Kagyu ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).
6. **Householder origins** -- Marpa was a householder (not a monk), and this lay practitioner tradition has continued alongside monastic practice ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)).

## Context & Application

Marpa Lotsawa trained as a translator and traveled three times to India and four times to Nepal in search of religious teachings ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)). Together Marpa, Milarepa, and Gampopa are known as "Mar-Mi-Dag Sum" and are considered the founders of the Kagyu school in Tibet ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)). The Kagyu school collaborated with the Nyingma in the 19th-century Rime movement; Mipham the Great's works became the foundation of study for not only the Nyingma but the Kagyu lineage as well ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([kagyu](/source/kagyu/)): The Mahamudra lineage of Tilopa and Naropa is called the "direct lineage" or "close lineage" as Tilopa is said to have received this realization directly from the Dharmakaya Buddha Vajradhara, and it was transmitted only through Naropa to Marpa.

## Relationships

- **Prerequisite: vajrayana** -- the Kagyu is a Vajrayana school.
- **Related to nyingma** -- the Kagyu and Nyingma share the Rime movement and some study traditions.
- **Related to mahamudra** -- Mahamudra is the principal meditative teaching of the Kagyu.
- **Related to rime-movement** -- the Kagyu school was instrumental in the 19th-century non-sectarian movement.
