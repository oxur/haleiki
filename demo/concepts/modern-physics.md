## Quick Definition

Modern physics refers to the post-classical era of physics beginning in the early 20th century, founded on quantum mechanics and the theory of relativity. It describes phenomena at extreme scales -- the very small, very fast, or very massive -- where classical physics fails.

## Core Definition

Modern physics began in the early 20th century with the work of Max Planck in quantum theory and Albert Einstein's theory of relativity. Both theories arose due to inaccuracies in classical mechanics in certain situations. The two chief theories present a different picture of space, time, and matter from that of classical physics: quantum theory addresses the discrete nature of phenomena at atomic and subatomic scales, while the theory of relativity describes phenomena in frames of reference moving at significant fractions of the speed of light or in the presence of strong gravitational fields ([physics](/source/physics/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Classical physics** -- modern physics is defined in contrast to and as an extension of classical physics; understanding what classical physics predicts (and where it fails) is essential.

## Key Properties

1. **Two foundational pillars** -- quantum mechanics and the theory of relativity ([physics](/source/physics/)).
2. **Extreme-scale applicability** -- concerns behavior of matter and energy under extreme conditions or at very large or very small scales ([physics](/source/physics/)).
3. **Conceptual revolution** -- fundamentally changed the concepts of space, time, matter, and causality.
4. **Key concepts** -- includes action, causality, covariance, quantum, symmetry, wave, and physical field ([physics](/source/physics/)).

## Context & Application

The failures of classical physics -- the null result of the Michelson-Morley experiment, blackbody radiation, and the photoelectric effect -- drove the development of relativity and quantum mechanics respectively. These theories have enabled technologies from nuclear energy and semiconductors to GPS and medical imaging ([physics](/source/physics/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([physics](/source/physics/)): Planck's solution to the blackbody radiation problem (1900) introduced the quantum hypothesis, marking the birth of quantum theory.

**Example 2** ([physics](/source/physics/)): Einstein's special relativity (1905) resolved the conflict between Newtonian mechanics and Maxwell's electromagnetism by replacing Galilean invariance with Lorentz invariance.
