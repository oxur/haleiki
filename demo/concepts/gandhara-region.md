## Quick Definition

Gandhara was an ancient Indo-Aryan civilization centered on the Peshawar Basin in present-day northwestern Pakistan and northeastern Afghanistan, renowned as a major center for Greco-Buddhism, Gandharan art, and as the origin of the Kharosthi script.

## Core Definition

Gandhara was an ancient Indo-Aryan civilization located in present-day northwestern Pakistan and northeastern Afghanistan. The core region was the Valley of Peshawar, with cultural influence extending across the Indus River to Taxila and westward into the Kabul Valley. Gandhara was a major center for Greco-Buddhism under the Indo-Greeks and Gandharan Buddhism under later dynasties, and a central location for the Silk Road transmission of Buddhism to Central and East Asia. Gandhari, an Indo-Aryan language written in Kharosthi script, served as the region's lingua franca ([gandhara](/source/gandhara/)). The Gandhara tribe is attested in the Rigveda (c. 1500-1200 BCE) and the region is mentioned in the Avesta as Vaēkərəta ([gandhara](/source/gandhara/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Language** -- Gandhara's significance partly lies in its linguistic and script traditions.

## Key Properties

1. **Crossroads civilization** -- situated at the intersection of Indian, Central Asian, and Middle Eastern cultures and trade routes.
2. **Kharosthi origin** -- birthplace of the Kharosthi script under Achaemenid rule via Aramaic influence.
3. **Greco-Buddhist art** -- developed a distinctive art style fusing Hellenistic classical forms with Buddhist iconography.
4. **Taxila** -- the capital Taxila was a major center of learning from the Vedic period onward.
5. **Long historical span** -- from the Gandhara grave culture (c. 1200 BCE) through the Hindu Shahi Kingdom (1001 CE).
6. **Buddhist transmission** -- central to the Silk Road transmission of Buddhism to East Asia.

## Context & Application

Gandhara was one of the sixteen Mahajanapadas ("great realms") of Iron Age South Asia. It was conquered by Cyrus I (6th century BCE), incorporated into the Achaemenid Empire, later conquered by Alexander the Great (327 BCE), and became part of the Maurya Empire. Under the Kushan Empire (1st-5th century CE), Gandhara flourished with its capital at Peshawar (Purushapura). The region declined after Alchon Hun invasions in the 6th century and the name disappeared after Mahmud of Ghazni's 1001 CE conquest ([gandhara](/source/gandhara/)).
