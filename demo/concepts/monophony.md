## Quick Definition

Monophony is a musical texture consisting of a single melodic line without accompaniment. It is the simplest and historically oldest type of musical texture.

## Core Definition

Monophonic texture consists of a single melodic voice, such as a piece for solo soprano or solo flute ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). During the earlier medieval period, liturgical music was monophonic chant, with Gregorian chant becoming the dominant style ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). The transition from monophonic to polyphonic music, beginning with organum in the 9th century, is one of the defining developments of Western music history.

## Key Properties

1. Consists of a single melodic line without accompaniment
2. The simplest and oldest type of musical texture
3. The dominant texture of early medieval music (Gregorian chant)
4. Contrasts with polyphony (multiple independent voices) and homophony (melody with chordal accompaniment)
5. Does not imply a single performer -- multiple voices or instruments may perform the same melody in unison or octaves

## Context & Application

Monophony was the primary musical texture of Western music before the development of polyphony in the 9th century. Gregorian chant is the most extensive surviving body of monophonic music from the medieval period ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): A piece for solo soprano or solo flute exemplifies monophonic texture -- a single melodic voice without accompaniment.

## Relationships

- **polyphony** -- Polyphony (multiple independent voices) developed from monophony
- **homophony** -- Homophony (melody with chordal accompaniment) contrasts with monophony
