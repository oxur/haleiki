## Quick Definition

Language is a structured system of communication consisting of grammar and vocabulary, used by humans to convey meaning in spoken, signed, and written forms. It is characterized by productivity, displacement, and cultural diversity.

## Core Definition

Language is a structured system of communication that consists of grammar and vocabulary. It is the primary means by which humans convey meaning, both in spoken and signed forms, and may also be conveyed through writing. Human language is characterized by productivity (the ability to create an infinite number of sentences from finite elements), displacement (the ability to refer to objects and events not immediately present), and cultural and historical diversity ([language](/source/language/)). The scientific study of language is called linguistics, and major figures include Ferdinand de Saussure and Noam Chomsky ([language](/source/language/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Productivity** -- humans can produce a vast range of utterances from a finite set of elements, creating an infinite number of larger units of meaning.
2. **Displacement** -- human language can refer to abstract concepts, imagined or hypothetical events, and events in the past or future.
3. **Dual code structure** -- a finite number of meaningless elements (sounds, letters, gestures) combine to form an infinite number of meaningful units (words, sentences).
4. **Modality independence** -- language can be conveyed through auditory (speech), visual (sign, writing), and tactile (braille) modalities.
5. **Social convention** -- the use of language relies on social convention and is acquired through learning.
6. **Arbitrariness** -- the relationship between linguistic signs and their meanings is based on social convention, not natural connection.

## Context & Application

Language is thought to have diverged from earlier primate communication systems when early hominins acquired theory of mind and shared intentionality. Estimates of the number of human languages vary between 5,000 and 7,000. Language is processed primarily in Broca's and Wernicke's areas of the brain. Languages evolve and diversify over time, and their history can be reconstructed by comparing modern languages via the comparative method. A group of languages descended from a common ancestor is called a language family ([language](/source/language/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([language](/source/language/)): Saussure's distinction between _langage_ (language as a general concept), _langue_ (a specific language system), and _parole_ (concrete speech in a particular language) illustrates the multiple levels at which language can be analyzed.

**Example 2** ([language](/source/language/)): The English word "language" derives from Proto-Indo-European *dn̥ǵʰweh₂s ("tongue, speech, language") through Latin _lingua_ and Old French _language_.
