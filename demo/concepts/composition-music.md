## Quick Definition

Musical composition is the process and art of creating music, encompassing the arrangement of musical elements including melody, harmony, rhythm, form, and texture into a coherent whole.

## Core Definition

Musical composition encompasses the methods and concepts that composers and other musicians use in creating music. Music theory as a practical discipline concerns itself with the development, preservation, and transmission of compositional practice ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The term "musical form" refers to the overall structure or plan of a piece of music, describing the layout of a composition as divided into sections ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). Composition involves orchestration, ornamentation, improvisation, and electronic sound production among other elements.

## Key Properties

1. The art and process of creating music
2. Involves organizing melody, harmony, rhythm, form, and texture
3. Musical form describes the structural plan of a composition
4. Encompasses both written (notated) and improvised creation
5. The techniques of tonal composition include harmony and counterpoint
6. Compositional methods vary across cultures and historical periods

## Context & Application

Composition is at the heart of all musical traditions. In Western music, it has developed from the anonymous creation of medieval chant through the increasingly individual art of the Renaissance, Baroque, Classical, and Romantic eras, to the diverse approaches of the 20th and 21st centuries. Music theory provides the conceptual framework for compositional practice ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): Percy Scholes defines musical form as "a series of strategies designed to find a successful mean between the opposite extremes of unrelieved repetition and unrelieved alteration."
