## Quick Definition

A language family is a group of languages that descend from a common ancestral language (proto-language), identified through systematic comparison of shared features using the comparative method.

## Core Definition

A group of languages that descend from a common ancestor is known as a language family. In contrast, a language that has been demonstrated not to have any living or non-living relationship with another language is called a language isolate ([language](/source/language/)). The Indo-European language family is the most extensively studied, with Proto-Indo-European as its reconstructed ancestor. Languages within a family diverge over time through the process of language change, as regional dialects undergo shifts in pronunciation, morphology, and vocabulary ([proto-indo-european](/source/proto-indo-european/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Common descent** -- all languages in a family derive from a single proto-language.
2. **Regular correspondences** -- systematic sound correspondences between related languages provide evidence of common descent.
3. **Branching structure** -- language families are modeled as tree-like structures of branching divergence.
4. **Reconstructible** -- the common ancestor can often be partially reconstructed using the comparative method.

## Context & Application

The Indo-European family, with Proto-Indo-European as its ancestor, is the most studied language family in the world. It includes Indo-Aryan (Sanskrit), Iranian (Avestan), Greek, Latin/Romance, Germanic, Celtic, Baltic, Slavic, and other branches. The Sino-Tibetan family includes Classical Tibetan. There are estimated to be 5,000-7,000 languages in the world, belonging to hundreds of families ([language](/source/language/)).
