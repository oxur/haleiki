## Quick Definition

Dzogchen ("Great Perfection" or "Great Completion") is a tradition of teachings in Indo-Tibetan Buddhism and Bon aimed at discovering and remaining in the ultimate ground of existence, called rigpa (knowledge or intrinsic awareness). It is considered the highest teaching in the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism.

## Core Definition

Dzogchen is a tradition of teachings aimed at discovering and remaining in the ultimate ground of existence; the goal is the direct experience of this basis, called rigpa (Sanskrit: vidya) ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). The term dzogchen is composed of rdzogs (perfection, completion) and chen (great), and initially referred to the "highest perfection" of Vajrayana deity yoga -- specifically, the stage after deity visualization has been dissolved and one rests in the natural state of the innately luminous and pure mind ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). Dzogchen emerged during the first dissemination of Buddhism in Tibet, around the 7th to 9th centuries CE; while considered a Tibetan development by some scholars, it draws upon key ideas from Indian sources ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). The earliest Dzogchen texts appeared in the 9th century, attributed to Indian masters like Sri Singha and Vimalamitra ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). Longchenpa (1308-1364) was the most important scholarly figure in the systematization of Dzogchen traditions, bringing together the Vima Nyingtik and Khandro Nyingtik teachings ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/), [longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)). The Nyingma school traditionally holds that its Dzogchen lineage has its origins in Garab Dorje through a lineage of transmission to Padmasambhava ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Nyingma** -- Dzogchen is the highest teaching within the Nyingma school; understanding the Nyingma tradition provides the necessary context of lineage, transmission, and practice framework.

## Key Properties

1. **Highest vehicle** -- considered the pinnacle of the nine yanas in the Nyingma classification system, also known as Atiyoga.
2. **Direct recognition of rigpa** -- focuses on the direct recognition of the nature of mind (rigpa, intrinsic awareness) rather than gradual cultivation; rigpa is "knowing, clear, and unchanging" ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
3. **Three series** -- classified into Semde (Mind Series), Longde (Space Series), and Menngagde (Instruction Series); the Instruction Series is the most widely practiced today ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
4. **Base, Path, Fruit** -- the Base represents the original state (emptiness, clarity, compassionate energy); the Path comprises view, practice, and conduct; the Fruit is realization of one's true nature ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
5. **Ground has three qualities** -- ngo bo (essence/emptiness), rang bzhin (nature/luminosity), and thugs rje (power/compassionate energy) ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
6. **Trekcho and togal** -- the two key Dzogchen meditation methods: trekcho ("cutting through") to recognize primordial purity, and togal ("direct crossing") involving visions through dark retreat or sky gazing ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
7. **Self-liberation** -- experiences are self-liberated through integration with the state of rigpa, in three degrees: cherdrol, shardrol, and rangdrol ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
8. **Rainbow body** -- advanced tögal practice may lead to the dissolution of the physical body into a rainbow body of light at death ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
9. **Lineage from Garab Dorje** -- the Dzogchen lineage is traced from Garab Dorje, whose three statements epitomize the teaching: direct introduction, deciding on one thing, and confidence in liberation ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
10. **Cross-school interest** -- Dzogchen teachings are not confined to the Nyingma; the Dalai Lamas' hidden lake temple (Lukhang) is dedicated to Dzogchen teachings ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)).

## Context & Application

Dzogchen arose in the era of the first dissemination of Buddhism in Tibet (7th-9th centuries) and continued during the Era of Fragmentation ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). Early Dzogchen was characterized by a departure from normative Vajrayana practice, focusing instead on simple calming contemplations leading to a direct immersion in awareness -- a "technique-free immersion in the bare immediacy of one's own deepest levels of awareness" ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). During the Tibetan renaissance era (10th-12th centuries), Dzogchen underwent significant development, incorporating new practices and teachings from India, including subtle body practices, visionary practices like dark retreat, and death-motif practices ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). The Seventeen Tantras became the most influential texts. Longchenpa's systematization in the 14th century was the turning point for Nyingma Dzogchen scholasticism ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). The Seven Line Prayer to Padmasambhava is said to contain the most sacred teachings of Dzogchen ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). Longchenpa transmitted the Longchen Nyingtik cycle of teachings and practice to Jikme Lingpa, and it has since become one of the most widely practiced traditions ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)): Garab Dorje's "Three Statements that Strike the Vital Point" epitomize the entire Dzogchen teaching: (1) Introducing directly the face of rigpa itself; (2) Deciding upon one thing and one thing only; (3) Confidence directly in the liberation of rising thoughts.

**Example 2** ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)): The Mirror of the Heart of Vajrasattva, a major Dzogchen tantra, explains that Dzogchen is "great" because it is the pinnacle of all vehicles, is "never moving from the natural state," and is "non-substantial rigpa beyond action and effort."

**Example 3** ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)): Longchenpa's Seven Treasuries were specifically written to elucidate the meaning of the seventeen main tantras of Dzogchen and the teachings of all nine yanas.

**Example 4** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): The Lukhang, the hidden lake temple of the Dalai Lamas behind the Potala Palace, is dedicated to Dzogchen teachings and has murals depicting the eight manifestations of Padmasambhava.

- **Related to longchen-nyingtik** -- the most widely practiced Dzogchen cycle today, revealed by Jigme Lingpa.
- **Related to trekcho** -- "cutting through" is one of the two key Dzogchen meditation methods.
- **Related to togal** -- "direct crossing" is the second key Dzogchen meditation method.

## Common Confusions

- **Dzogchen as "doing nothing"** -- while Dzogchen emphasizes non-fabrication and naturalness, it includes a full range of practices from preliminaries to advanced methods; Jigme Lingpa himself states that for most people, a gradual path of training is needed ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
- **Simultaneous vs. gradual approaches** -- there is a tension between methods emphasizing primordial liberation and methods emphasizing gradual practice; authors of the tradition explain this as relating to different levels of practitioner ability ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).
