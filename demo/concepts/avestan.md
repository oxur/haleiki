## Quick Definition

Avestan is the extinct liturgical language of Zoroastrianism, belonging to the Iranian branch of the Indo-European language family. Old Avestan is remarkably close to Vedic Sanskrit, reflecting their recent divergence from a common Indo-Iranian ancestor.

## Core Definition

Avestan is the liturgical language of Zoroastrianism, belonging to the Iranian branch of the Indo-European language family, originally spoken during the Avestan period (c. 1500-400 BCE) in eastern Greater Iran. It falls into two groups: Old Avestan (c. 1500-900 BCE) and Younger Avestan (c. 900-400 BCE). Old Avestan is quite close in both grammar and lexicon to Vedic Sanskrit, the oldest preserved Indo-Aryan language, indicating only a limited period since their separation from their common Proto-Indo-Iranian ancestor ([avestan](/source/avestan/)). The region of Gandhara is mentioned in the Avesta as Vaēkərəta ([gandhara](/source/gandhara/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Language** -- Avestan is a language.
- **Proto-Indo-European** -- Avestan's significance lies in its relationship to PIE through Proto-Indo-Iranian.

## Key Properties

1. **Two variants** -- Old Avestan (archaic, Gathas) and Younger Avestan (later texts, Yashts, Vendidad).
2. **Liturgical preservation** -- texts were transmitted orally, then written down during the Sasanian period (c. 400-500 CE).
3. **Close to Vedic Sanskrit** -- Old Avestan largely agrees morphologically with Vedic Sanskrit.
4. **Dedicated alphabet** -- the Avestan alphabet has 53 characters designed for high phonetic precision in liturgical recitation.
5. **No living descendants** -- none of the known Middle Iranian languages are successors of Avestan.
6. **Known only from the Avesta** -- the language is otherwise unattested outside the Avesta corpus.

## Context & Application

The Gathas (Old Avestan) show strong linguistic and cultural similarities with the Rigveda, both representing the second half of the second millennium BCE. The correct enunciation of Avestan liturgies was considered necessary for prayers to be effective, motivating the creation of a highly precise alphabet. Zoroastrians of India also transcribe Avestan in Brahmi-based scripts ([avestan](/source/avestan/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([sanskrit](/source/sanskrit/)): The Rigvedic hymns are "notably similar to the most archaic poems of the Iranian ... language families, the Gathas of old Avestan."

**Example 2** ([avestan](/source/avestan/)): The name "Avestan" derives from the Avesta, the written collection of texts, from Persian _avestā_, possibly originating from a hypothetical Avestan term *upastāvaka (praise song).
