## Quick Definition

The Proto-Sinaitic script is a Middle Bronze Age writing system (c. 19th-16th century BC) from the Sinai Peninsula and Middle Egypt, recognized as the earliest known alphabetic writing and the ancestor of most modern alphabets including Phoenician, Greek, and Latin.

## Core Definition

The Proto-Sinaitic script is a Middle Bronze Age writing system known from about 30-40 inscriptions from Serabit el-Khadim in the Sinai Peninsula and two inscriptions from Wadi el-Hol in Middle Egypt. Together with Proto-Canaanite inscriptions, it represents the earliest trace of alphabetic writing and the common ancestor of the Phoenician alphabet, which led to the Greek alphabet and subsequently the Latin alphabet ([proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)). Alphabetic writing first appeared c. 1800 BC to write a Semitic language spoken in the Sinai Peninsula, and most of the world's alphabets either descend directly from this script or were inspired by its design ([writing-system](/source/writing-system/)).

- **Writing system** -- understanding the classification of writing systems (alphabets, syllabaries, logographies) is needed to appreciate Proto-Sinaitic's significance as the first alphabet.

## Key Properties

1. **Earliest alphabet** -- the earliest known alphabetic writing system, dating to c. 1850-1550 BC.
2. **Egyptian hieroglyph derivation** -- letters derive from Egyptian hieroglyphs via the acrophonic principle (e.g., house hieroglyph → /b/ from _bayt_, "house").
3. **Abjad type** -- a consonantal alphabet (abjad), writing primarily consonant sounds.
4. **Ancestor of most modern alphabets** -- gave rise to Phoenician → Greek → Latin and many other scripts.
5. **Small corpus** -- known from only about 50-60 inscriptions total.
6. **Multiple discovery sites** -- Serabit el-Khadim (Sinai turquoise mines) and Wadi el-Hol (Egyptian desert road).

## Context & Application

The inscriptions were first discovered by Hilda and Flinders Petrie in the winter of 1904-1905 at Serabit el-Khadim. Alan Gardiner in 1916 identified the word _bʿlt_ ("Lady," referring to the goddess Hathor) and assigned sound values by assuming the signs represented a Semitic abjad. The Wadi el-Hol inscriptions (discovered 1999) suggest the script originated in Egypt itself, between 1900 and 1800 BC ([proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)): The character for /b/ derives from the Egyptian hieroglyph for "house" (pr), adopted via the Semitic word _bayt_ (house) -- the acrophonic principle.

**Example 2** ([proto-sinaitic-script](/source/proto-sinaitic-script/)): The recurring word _lbʿlt_ ("to the Lady") in Serabit inscriptions, referring to Hathor, was Gardiner's key to decipherment.
