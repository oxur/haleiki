## Quick Definition

Cool jazz is a style of modern jazz that emerged in the late 1940s, characterized by relaxed tempos, a lighter tone, and formal arrangements incorporating elements of classical music. It arose as a reaction to the fast, complex bebop style.

## Core Definition

Cool jazz is a style and genre of modern jazz music inspired by bebop and big band that arose in the United States after World War II. It is characterized by relaxed tempos and a lighter tone than that used in the fast and complex bebop style. Cool jazz often employs formal arrangements and incorporates elements of classical music ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)). The term "cool" started being applied to this music around 1953. Cool jazz developed near the end of the 1940s, introducing calmer, smoother sounds and long, linear melodic lines ([jazz](/source/jazz/)).

## Key Properties

1. Emerged in the late 1940s as a reaction to bebop
2. Characterized by relaxed tempos and lighter, more subdued tone
3. Often incorporates formal arrangements and classical music elements
4. Four subcategories: soft variants of bebop, advanced swing-era developments, West Coast jazz, and exploratory subdued music
5. Key antecedent: saxophonist Lester Young's light sound and melodic improvisation
6. Stylistic origins in bebop, swing, classical, and third stream music

## Context & Application

Cool jazz emerged as a gentler alternative to the intensity of bebop. Miles Davis's Birth of the Cool recordings (1949-50) are foundational to the genre. The style was particularly associated with the West Coast, though it had important New York practitioners as well. Bix Beiderbecke and Lester Young are identified as early progenitors of the cool aesthetic ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)): The Miles Davis recordings that constitute Birth of the Cool represent "soft variants of bebop," the first subcategory of cool jazz identified by Mark C. Gridley.

**Example 2** ([cool-jazz](/source/cool-jazz/)): Dave Brubeck and Paul Desmond exemplify the second subcategory -- modern players who eschewed bebop in favor of advanced swing-era developments.

## Relationships

- **miles-davis-musician** -- Davis's Birth of the Cool sessions were foundational to the genre
- **modal-jazz** -- Modal jazz developed as a derivative form of cool jazz
- **bebop** -- Cool jazz arose as a reaction to bebop's intensity and complexity

## Common Confusions

- **Cool jazz vs. West Coast jazz**: West Coast jazz is a subset of cool jazz, not a synonym. Cool jazz also had important East Coast practitioners.
- **Cool jazz vs. smooth jazz**: Smooth jazz is a later commercial form (1980s) distinct from the 1940s-50s cool jazz movement.
