## Quick Definition

Vajrayana ("diamond vehicle" or "thunderbolt vehicle") is a form of tantric Buddhism that employs rituals, mantras, visualization, and esoteric practices as a path to enlightenment. It is the dominant form of Buddhism in Tibet, Bhutan, Nepal, and the Himalayan regions.

## Core Definition

Vajrayana is the tantric tradition of Buddhism that Padmasambhava is credited with fully revealing in Tibet circa the 8th-9th centuries ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). It encompasses esoteric practices including mantra recitation, deity visualization, ritual empowerment, and the use of tantric texts. Padmasambhava is said to have spread Vajrayana Buddhism to the people of Tibet and specifically introduced its practice of tantra ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). The Vajrayana tradition includes both the old translation (Nyingma) and new translation (Sarma) schools of Tibetan Buddhism. Longchenpa received the corpus of both old and new translation tantras during his extensive studies ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Tantric methods** -- employs rituals, mantras, deity visualization, and empowerment as a path to enlightenment.
2. **Transmission lineage** -- teachings are transmitted through a lineage from teacher to student, including oral transmissions (kama) and hidden treasure texts (terma).
3. **Multiple schools** -- encompassed by multiple schools of Tibetan Buddhism, including Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, and Gelug.
4. **Esoteric and exoteric** -- includes both openly taught and secret practices requiring initiation.

## Context & Application

According to traditional accounts, Padmasambhava fully revealed the Vajrayana in Tibet, having been invited by King Trisong Detsen to help found Samye monastery ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)). The tradition continued to develop through the works of scholars like Longchenpa, who received and synthesized both old and new translation tantras ([longchenpa](/source/longchenpa/)). Vajrayana practices are central to all schools of Tibetan Buddhism and to the Buddhist traditions of Bhutan, Nepal, and the Himalayan regions.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): Padmasambhava taught various forms of tantric Buddhist yoga in Tibet and initiated King Trisong Detsen into tantric rites.

**Example 2** ([padmasambhava](/source/padmasambhava/)): The Vajra Guru mantra -- "Om ah hum vajra guru padma siddhi hum" -- is a Vajrayana practice held to engender communion with the Three Vajras of Padmasambhava's mindstream.

## Relationships

- **Related to nyingma** -- the Nyingma school is the oldest Vajrayana lineage in Tibet, founded by Padmasambhava.
- **Related to dzogchen** -- Dzogchen is considered the highest teaching within the Vajrayana, particularly in the Nyingma tradition.
- **Related to padmasambhava-teacher** -- Padmasambhava is credited with introducing Vajrayana to Tibet.
