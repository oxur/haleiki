## Quick Definition

Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche (c. 1910-1991) was a Vajrayana master, terton, scholar, poet, and head of the Nyingma school from 1988 to 1991. He was the primary custodian of the vast collection of teachings authored by and recovered by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo, and a leading proponent of the Rime tradition.

## Core Definition

Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche was a Vajrayana master, terton, scholar, poet, teacher, and recognized by Buddhists as one of the greatest realized masters ([dilgo-khyentse](/source/dilgo-khyentse/)). As the primary custodian of the vast collection of teachings both authored by and recovered by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo, he was the de facto custodian of a vast majority of Tibetan Buddhist teachings. He taught many eminent teachers, including the 14th Dalai Lama ([dilgo-khyentse](/source/dilgo-khyentse/)). After the Chinese invasion of Tibet, his personal effort was crucial in the preservation of Tibetan Buddhism ([dilgo-khyentse](/source/dilgo-khyentse/)).

4. **Terton** -- recognized as a treasure revealer who discovered and transmitted concealed teachings.
5. **Teacher of teachers** -- taught many eminent masters including the 14th Dalai Lama ([dilgo-khyentse](/source/dilgo-khyentse/)).

## Context & Application

Born in the Denma region of Derge in Kham, eastern Tibet, Dilgo Khyentse's family directly descended from Tibet's eighth-century King Trisong Detsen ([dilgo-khyentse](/source/dilgo-khyentse/)). He published a thirty-six-volume edition of the Nyingma Gyubum in New Delhi in 1974 ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). His efforts to preserve Tibetan Buddhist teachings after the Chinese invasion were considered crucial. He was recognized as a reincarnation of Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo, a key figure in the 19th-century Rime movement ([dilgo-khyentse](/source/dilgo-khyentse/)).
