## Quick Definition

The motet is one of the most important polyphonic musical forms of the medieval and Renaissance periods. It evolved from the clausulae of Notre-Dame organum and became a vehicle for both sacred and secular texts in multiple voices.

## Core Definition

The motet, one of the most important musical forms of the high Middle Ages and Renaissance, developed initially during the Notre-Dame period out of the clausula ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). While most early motets were sacred, by the end of the thirteenth century the genre expanded to include secular topics. Motets included from one to three upper voices, each with its own text ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). In the Renaissance, the motet continued as a principal liturgical form, with Palestrina composing more than 250 examples ([palestrina](/source/palestrina/)).

## Prerequisites

- **polyphony** -- The motet is inherently polyphonic, requiring understanding of multi-voice texture
- **counterpoint** -- Motet composition relies on contrapuntal technique

## Key Properties

1. Polyphonic vocal form, typically for three or more voices
2. Evolved from clausulae in Notre-Dame organum
3. Originally sacred, later expanded to include secular texts
4. Multiple simultaneous texts (polytextual) in medieval examples
5. One of the most important genres of both medieval and Renaissance periods
6. Tenor voice typically carried a pre-existing chant melody (cantus firmus)

## Context & Application

The motet evolved from sacred origins to encompass secular and political subject matter during the medieval period, then returned to primarily sacred use during the Renaissance under the influence of the Counter-Reformation ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/), [renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)): Medieval motets from the Notre-Dame school featured multiple voices with different texts sung simultaneously over a slow-moving tenor derived from chant.

**Example 2** ([palestrina](/source/palestrina/)): Palestrina composed more than 250 motets, making it one of his most prolific genres alongside masses.

## Relationships

- **organum** -- The motet evolved from the clausulae sections of Notre-Dame organum
- **gregorian-chant** -- Chant melodies served as the cantus firmus foundation for many motets
- **palestrina-composer** -- Palestrina was one of the most prolific composers of motets
