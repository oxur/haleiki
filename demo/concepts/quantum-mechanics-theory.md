## Quick Definition

Quantum mechanics is the fundamental physical theory that describes the behavior of matter and light at and below the atomic scale. It replaces classical mechanics in the microscopic regime and is the foundation of all quantum physics.

## Core Definition

Quantum mechanics is the fundamental physical theory that describes the behavior of matter and of light; its unusual characteristics typically occur at and below the scale of atoms. It is the foundation of all quantum physics, which includes quantum chemistry, quantum biology, quantum field theory, quantum technology, and quantum information science ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)). Classical physics can describe many aspects of nature at ordinary scales but is insufficient for describing phenomena at submicroscopic scales. Classical mechanics can be derived from quantum mechanics as an approximation valid at ordinary scales ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Probabilistic predictions** -- quantum mechanics usually cannot predict with certainty what will happen, only giving probabilities via the Born rule ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).
2. **Quantization** -- bound quantum systems have discrete values of energy, momentum, and angular momentum, in contrast to classical systems where these quantities are continuous.
3. **Wave-particle duality** -- measurements show characteristics of both particles and waves.
4. **Uncertainty principle** -- there are fundamental limits to how precisely conjugate quantities (e.g., position and momentum) can be simultaneously predicted.
5. **Entanglement** -- quantum systems can become intertwined so that a description of the whole is not reducible to descriptions of the individual parts.
6. **Extreme accuracy** -- predictions have been verified to within 1 part in $10^{12}$ (e.g., QED predictions of electron magnetic properties) ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).

## Context & Application

Quantum mechanics arose gradually from theories explaining observations incompatible with classical physics: Planck's 1900 solution to the black-body radiation problem, Einstein's 1905 photoelectric effect paper, and Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom. Modern quantum mechanics was developed in the mid-1920s by Bohr, Schrodinger, Heisenberg, Born, Dirac, and others. Applications span quantum chemistry, quantum computing, semiconductors, lasers, MRI imaging, and the Standard Model of particle physics ([quantum-mechanics](/source/quantum-mechanics/)).
