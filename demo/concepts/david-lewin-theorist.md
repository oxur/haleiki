## Quick Definition

David Lewin (1933-2003) was an American music theorist, critic, and composer called "the most original and far-ranging theorist of his generation." He developed transformational theory, which applies mathematical group theory to music.

## Core Definition

David Benjamin Lewin was an American music theorist, music critic and composer. Called "the most original and far-ranging theorist of his generation," he did his most influential theoretical work on the development of transformational theory, which involves the application of mathematical group theory to music ([david-lewin](/source/david-lewin/)). Transformational theory is a branch of music theory developed by Lewin in the 1980s, and formally introduced in his 1987 work, Generalized Musical Intervals and Transformations. The theory models musical transformations as elements of a mathematical group and can be used to analyze both tonal and atonal music ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

6. Walter W. Naumburg Professor of Music at Harvard (1985-2003)

## Context & Application

Lewin's transformational theory changed the focus of music analysis from musical objects to relations between objects. Instead of describing a C major chord followed by a G major chord, a transformational theorist might describe the first chord as "transformed" into the second by the "Dominant operation" ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). The theory can be applied to pitch, rhythm and metre, or even timbre, and works for both tonal and atonal repertories ([david-lewin](/source/david-lewin/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)): Transformational theory models the progression from C major to G major not as two discrete objects but as the application of a "Dominant operation" -- symbolically: Dominant(C major) = G major.

**Example 2** ([david-lewin](/source/david-lewin/)): In Musical Form and Transformation: Four Analytic Essays, Lewin provides ear-training exercises to help readers hear the mathematical relationships his theory describes.
