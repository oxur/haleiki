## Quick Definition

Deity yoga (devatayoga) is the fundamental practice of Buddhist Tantra, involving meditation on a chosen deity (yidam) through visualization, mantra recitation, and identification with the deity's enlightened qualities. According to Tsongkhapa, deity yoga is what separates Tantra from Sutra practice.

## Core Definition

The fundamental practice of Buddhist Tantra is "deity yoga" (devatayoga), meditation on a chosen deity or "cherished divinity" (Sanskrit: Ista-devata, Tibetan: yidam), which involves the recitation of mantras and prayers and visualization of the deity, the associated mandala of the deity's Buddha field, along with consorts and attendant Buddhas and bodhisattvas ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)). In the Unsurpassed Yoga Tantras, this method is divided into two stages: generation (utpatti-krama) and completion (nispanna-krama) ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)). The deity is to be imagined as "empty yet apparent," with the character of a mirage or a rainbow, combined with "divine pride" -- the thought that one is oneself the deity being visualized ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Vajrayana** -- deity yoga is a specifically Vajrayana practice requiring empowerment and understanding of tantric principles.

## Key Properties

1. **Two stages** -- generation stage (creating the visualization) and completion stage (dissolving into emptiness and working with subtle body) ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).
2. **Requires empowerment** -- only those who receive an empowerment or initiation may practice deity yoga ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).
3. **Empty yet apparent** -- the visualization is not solid or tangible; the deity is imagined like a mirage or rainbow ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).
4. **Divine pride** -- the practitioner cultivates the thought of being the deity, based on compassion and understanding of emptiness ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).
5. **Mandala context** -- deity visualization occurs within the context of the deity's mandala, a symbolic depiction of the sacred space ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).

## Context & Application

Deity yoga is practiced across all schools of Tibetan Buddhism. The foremost deities (yidams) practiced by Nyingma masters are Vajrakila (Dorje Phurba) and Vajra Heruka ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). In Tibetan Buddhism, advanced practices like deity yoga are usually preceded by preliminary practices called ngondro ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)). The practice uses the "three mysteries" -- affiliating body, speech, and mind with those of a Buddha through mudra, mantras, and samadhi ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([vajrayana](/source/vajrayana/)): In the generation stage, one dissolves one's reality into emptiness and meditates on the deity-mandala, resulting in identification with divine reality. The divine image is then dissolved into luminous emptiness, followed by re-emergence as the deity.

## Relationships

- **Prerequisite: vajrayana** -- deity yoga is the defining practice of Vajrayana Buddhism.
- **Related to nyingma** -- the Nyingma school practices deity yoga with particular deities like Vajrakila.
- **Related to dzogchen** -- Dzogchen is sometimes contrasted with deity yoga as a more direct method.
