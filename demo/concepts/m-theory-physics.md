## Quick Definition

M-theory is a conjectured eleven-dimensional theory that unifies all five consistent versions of superstring theory. Proposed by Edward Witten in 1995, it is approximated at low energies by eleven-dimensional supergravity and describes two- and five-dimensional branes.

## Core Definition

M-theory is a theory that unifies all consistent versions of superstring theory. Edward Witten first conjectured its existence in 1995, initiating the second superstring revolution. Prior to Witten's announcement, string theorists had identified five versions of superstring theory that were related by S-duality and T-duality. M-theory lives in eleven spacetime dimensions and is approximated at low energies by eleven-dimensional supergravity ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)). A complete formulation of M-theory is not yet known; the "M" may stand for "magic", "mystery", or "membrane" according to Witten ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).

## Prerequisites

- **String theory** -- M-theory unifies the five superstring theories; understanding string theory is essential.
- **Supersymmetry** -- M-theory is fundamentally supersymmetric; its eleven-dimensional limit is supergravity.
- **Supergravity** -- eleven-dimensional supergravity is the low-energy limit of M-theory.

## Key Properties

1. **Eleven-dimensional** -- ten spatial dimensions plus one time dimension ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).
2. **Unifies five superstring theories** -- type I, type IIA, type IIB, and two heterotic theories are all limiting cases ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).
3. **Branes** -- describes two-dimensional membranes and five-dimensional branes ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).
4. **Low-energy limit** -- eleven-dimensional supergravity ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).
5. **No complete formulation** -- candidate approaches include the BFSS matrix model and AdS/CFT ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).
6. **Dualities** -- S-duality and T-duality connect the various limiting cases ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).

## Context & Application

M-theory emerged from the convergence of several developments: S-duality and T-duality relating different string theories, the discovery that eleven-dimensional supergravity membranes reproduce type IIA strings upon compactification, and the theoretical elegance of eleven dimensions as the maximum for consistent supergravity (Nahm, 1978; Cremmer-Julia-Scherk, 1978). Witten's 1995 announcement unified these results. M-theory provides frameworks for studying quantum gravity, black hole physics, and has connections to pure mathematics through the AdS/CFT correspondence and the (2,0)-theory ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([m-theory](/source/m-theory/)): A membrane in eleven-dimensional supergravity, when wrapped around a compactified circular dimension, reproduces exactly the strings of type IIA superstring theory.

**Example 2** ([string-theory](/source/string-theory/)): The BFSS matrix model describes M-theory in terms of nine large matrices; its low-energy limit reproduces eleven-dimensional supergravity.
