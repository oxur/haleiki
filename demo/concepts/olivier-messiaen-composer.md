## Quick Definition

Olivier Messiaen (1908-1992) was a French composer, organist, and ornithologist. One of the major composers of the 20th century, he developed the modes of limited transposition, incorporated birdsong into his music, and was an outstanding teacher whose students included Boulez, Stockhausen, and Xenakis.

## Core Definition

Olivier Eugene Prosper Charles Messiaen was a French composer, organist, and ornithologist. One of the major composers of the 20th century, he was also an outstanding teacher of composition and musical analysis ([olivier-messiaen](/source/olivier-messiaen/)). His many distinguished pupils included Iannis Xenakis, Pierre Boulez, Karlheinz Stockhausen, and George Benjamin. Messiaen's style drew on Debussy's harmonic innovations ([claude-debussy](/source/claude-debussy/)) while incorporating novel rhythmic techniques, modes of limited transposition, and transcriptions of birdsong.

## Key Properties

1. Born in Avignon (1908), died in Clichy (1992)
2. Organist at the Eglise de la Sainte-Trinite, Paris, for 61 years
3. Developed the modes of limited transposition
4. Incorporated birdsong transcriptions into his music
5. His students included Boulez, Stockhausen, Xenakis, and Kurtag
6. Composed the Quatuor pour la fin du temps while a prisoner of war

## Context & Application

Messiaen was a bridge between Debussy's innovations and the post-war avant-garde. His teaching at the Paris Conservatoire shaped an entire generation of composers. The Quatuor pour la fin du temps, composed and first performed in a German prisoner-of-war camp during World War II, is one of the most remarkable works of the 20th century ([olivier-messiaen](/source/olivier-messiaen/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([olivier-messiaen](/source/olivier-messiaen/)): The Quatuor pour la fin du temps (Quartet for the End of Time) was composed for the four instruments available in Stalag VIII-A -- piano, violin, cello and clarinet -- and first performed for an audience of inmates and prison guards.
