## Quick Definition

Kharosthi is an ancient Indic abugida script developed in the Gandhara region (5th-3rd century BCE), derived from the Aramaic alphabet. Written right-to-left, it was used for Gandhari Prakrit and Buddhist texts until it died out around the 3rd-5th century CE.

## Core Definition

The Kharosthi script, also known as the Gandhari script, was an ancient Indic script originally developed in the Gandhara region of the northwestern Indian subcontinent, between the 5th and 3rd century BCE. An abugida derived from the Aramaic alphabet with extensive modifications, it was used primarily by the people of Gandhara in South Asia and Central Asia, along the Silk Road ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/)). It was during Achaemenid rule of Gandhara that the Kharosthi script was born through contact with the Aramaic alphabet ([gandhara](/source/gandhara/)). Some of the Edicts of Ashoka were written in Kharosthi ([edicts-of-ashoka](/source/edicts-of-ashoka/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Aramaic derivation** -- derived from the Aramaic alphabet, likely arriving with the Achaemenid conquest of the Indus Valley (c. 500 BCE).
2. **Abugida type** -- consonant-vowel pairs with inherent vowels, modified by diacritics.
3. **Right-to-left** -- unlike most Indic scripts, Kharosthi is written right to left.
4. **Arapacana order** -- follows its own unique letter order (a-ra-pa-ca-na), used as a Buddhist mnemonic.
5. **Gandharan Buddhist texts** -- the oldest Buddhist manuscripts (1st century BCE-3rd century CE) are written in Kharosthi on birch bark.
6. **Extinct** -- died out around the 3rd-5th century CE, replaced by Brahmi-derived scripts.

## Context & Application

Kharosthi was deciphered in 1835-36 by James Prinsep and Carl Ludwig Grotefend using bilingual coins of the Indo-Greek Kingdom (Greek obverse, Pali/Kharosthi reverse). This led to reading the Edicts of Ashoka at Mansehra and Shahbazgarhi. The discovery of Gandharan Buddhist texts -- birch bark manuscripts donated to the British Library in 1994 -- reinvigorated Kharosthi studies ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/)): The arapacana alphabet was used in Gandharan Buddhism as a mnemonic for the Prajnaparamita Sutra, with the first line preserved in the mantra of Manjushri.

**Example 2** ([kharosthi](/source/kharosthi/)): The words "inscription of the dharma" (_dhrāma dipu_) in Ashoka's Major Rock Edict demonstrate Kharosthi's use for royal inscriptions.
