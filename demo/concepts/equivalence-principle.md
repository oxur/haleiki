## Quick Definition

The equivalence principle states that gravitational and inertial effects are locally indistinguishable: an observer in a sealed room cannot tell whether the room is stationary in a gravitational field or accelerating in free space. This principle was Einstein's crucial guiding insight in developing general relativity.

## Core Definition

The equivalence principle arises from the universality of free fall: the trajectory of a test body in free fall depends only on its position and initial speed, but not on any of its material properties. There is no observable distinction between inertial motion and motion under gravity. The Einstein equivalence principle generalizes this: the laws of special relativity hold to good approximation in freely falling (and non-rotating) reference frames. This principle was the crucial guiding principle for generalizing special-relativistic physics to include gravity ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Local indistinguishability** -- gravitational and inertial effects cannot be distinguished locally ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
2. **Universal free fall** -- all bodies fall at the same rate regardless of composition (Eotvos experiments) ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
3. **Special relativity in free fall** -- the laws of special relativity hold in freely falling reference frames ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
4. **Motivates curved spacetime** -- the equivalence principle implies that gravity is not a force but a manifestation of spacetime curvature ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Context & Application

Einstein began with the equivalence principle as a thought experiment in 1907 (the "elevator experiment") and spent eight years developing it into general relativity. The principle is confirmed by experiments from Eotvos (1890s) to modern torsion balance tests. It is what distinguishes gravity from all other forces: gravity couples universally to all forms of energy ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): Einstein's elevator: an observer in a sealed room cannot distinguish between being stationary on Earth's surface and being accelerated at 9.8 m/s^2 in free space.
