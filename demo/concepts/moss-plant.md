## Quick Definition

Mosses are small, non-vascular flowerless plants in the division Bryophyta that reproduce via spores rather than seeds. They typically form dense green clumps in damp or shady locations and are distinguished by having the haploid gametophyte as the dominant life-cycle stage.

## Core Definition

Mosses are small, non-vascular flowerless plants in the taxonomic division Bryophyta sensu stricto ([moss](/source/moss/)). They typically form dense green clumps or mats, often in damp or shady locations. Individual plants are composed of simple leaves generally only one cell thick, attached to a stem with limited role in conducting water and nutrients. Mosses do not have seeds; after fertilization they develop sporophytes with unbranched stalks topped with single capsules containing spores. There are approximately 12,000 species ([moss](/source/moss/)). The haploid gametophyte generation is dominant, contrasting with the pattern in all vascular plants where the diploid sporophyte is dominant.

## Key Properties

1. Non-vascular: lack true xylem tracheids or vessels
2. Approximately 12,000 species, divided into eight classes
3. Haploid gametophyte is the dominant life-cycle stage
4. Reproduce via spores, not seeds
5. Typically 0.2-10 cm tall; tallest (Dawsonia superba) can reach 60 cm
6. Absorb 6.4 billion tons of carbon annually
7. Keystone species important for habitat restoration, reforestation, and erosion control
8. Sperm require water for fertilization (biflagellate)

## Context & Application

Mosses are ecologically significant as carbon sinks, pioneer organisms, and keystone species. Peat mosses (Sphagnum) are the main constituent of peat and commercially significant. Ancient moss ancestors (~470 million years ago) may have triggered the Ordovician ice ages by absorbing CO2 and chemically weathering rocks ([moss](/source/moss/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([moss](/source/moss/)): Sphagnum mosses form extensive acidic bogs; their dead cells store water, and their sporangia eject spores at 36,000 times gravitational acceleration.

**Example 2** ([moss](/source/moss/)): Physcomitrium patens is a model organism for DNA repair research, with its genome fully sequenced.

## Relationships

- **Angiosperm** contrasts with mosses as vascular seed plants vs. non-vascular spore plants
- **Lichen** is often confused with moss but is unrelated (fungal symbiosis, not a plant)
- **Gametophyte/sporophyte** alternation is the key life-cycle pattern, with the gametophyte dominant in mosses

## Common Confusions

- **Mosses are not lichens**: "Reindeer moss" and "Iceland moss" are actually lichens, not mosses
- **Mosses are not entirely non-vascular**: Some mosses have advanced conducting tissues, though structurally different from vascular plant vessels
