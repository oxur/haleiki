## Quick Definition

Johann Sebastian Bach (1685-1750) was a German composer and musician of the late Baroque period, widely regarded as one of the greatest composers of Western classical music. He is known for his mastery of counterpoint, fugue, and harmonic organization.

## Core Definition

Johann Sebastian Bach was a German composer and musician of the late Baroque period. He is known for his prolific output across a variety of instruments and forms, including the orchestral Brandenburg Concertos, the Cello Suites, keyboard works such as the Goldberg Variations and The Well-Tempered Clavier, and choral works such as the St. Matthew Passion and the Mass in B minor. He is known for his mastery of counterpoint, as heard in The Musical Offering and The Art of Fugue ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)). Bach enriched established German styles through his mastery of counterpoint, harmonic and motivic organisation, and his adaptation of rhythms, forms, and textures from abroad, particularly Italy and France ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)).

## Key Properties

1. Born in Eisenach (1685), died in Leipzig (1750)
2. Prolific output: hundreds of cantatas, masses, passions, oratorios, keyboard works, orchestral works
3. Supreme master of counterpoint, fugue, and canon
4. Served as Thomaskantor (church music director) in Leipzig from 1723
5. His music synthesized German, Italian, and French styles
6. Felix Mendelssohn's 1829 performance of the St. Matthew Passion launched the Bach Revival
7. Four of his twenty children became notable composers

## Context & Application

Bach was not widely known as a composer during his lifetime, being primarily recognized as an organist. The 19th-century Bach Revival, initiated by Mendelssohn, established him as one of the greatest composers in history. His contrapuntal techniques and harmonic language have profoundly influenced all subsequent Western music ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)): The Well-Tempered Clavier demonstrates Bach's keyboard mastery with 48 preludes and fugues in all major and minor keys.

**Example 2** ([johann-sebastian-bach](/source/johann-sebastian-bach/)): The Art of Fugue represents the pinnacle of contrapuntal technique, exploring virtually every possibility of fugal writing from a single musical subject.
