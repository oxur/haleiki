## Quick Definition

Musical minimalism is a compositional movement originating in the 1960s, characterized by repetitive structures, steady pulse, consonant harmony, and gradual processes of change. Philip Glass, Steve Reich, and Terry Riley are its founding figures.

## Core Definition

Musical minimalism (or minimal music) is a compositional approach that emerged in the 1960s, characterized by the use of repetitive structures, steady pulse, consonant harmony, and gradual processes of transformation. Philip Glass is known for his work in minimal music ([philip-glass](/source/philip-glass/)). Arvo Part's tintinnabuli technique is associated with a variant often called "sacred minimalism," which shares the reduction of materials but differs in its spiritual orientation and compositional technique ([arvo-part](/source/arvo-part/)).

## Prerequisites

- **harmony** -- Minimalism typically employs consonant, tonal harmony
- **rhythm** -- Rhythmic repetition and additive processes are central to the minimalist approach

## Key Properties

1. Emerged in the 1960s, primarily in the United States
2. Characterized by repetitive patterns and gradual transformation
3. Typically employs consonant, tonal harmony
4. Steady pulse is a defining feature
5. Key founders: Philip Glass, Steve Reich, Terry Riley, La Monte Young
6. Has spawned post-minimalism and influenced popular and film music

## Context & Application

Minimalism arose partly in reaction to the complexity of post-war serialism and twelve-tone music. Its accessible, tonal language helped bridge the gap between contemporary classical music and popular audiences. The movement's influence extends beyond classical music into ambient, electronic, and film scoring traditions ([philip-glass](/source/philip-glass/), [arvo-part](/source/arvo-part/)).
