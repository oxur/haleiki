## Quick Definition

John Coltrane (1926-1967) was an American jazz saxophonist and composer known for his technical virtuosity, his "sheets of sound" approach, and his pioneering work in hard bop, modal jazz, and free jazz.

## Core Definition

John William Coltrane was an American jazz saxophonist and composer. Known also as "Trane," he was one of the most influential musicians in jazz history ([john-coltrane](/source/john-coltrane/)). His genres encompassed jazz, hard bop, and free jazz. His landmark album A Love Supreme is widely regarded as one of the greatest jazz recordings ever made. He was a key member of Miles Davis's influential groups before leading his own groundbreaking ensembles.

## Key Properties

1. Born in Hamlet, North Carolina (1926), died in Huntington, New York (1967)
2. Developed the "sheets of sound" technique -- rapid, densely packed note sequences
3. A Love Supreme (1964) is one of the most acclaimed jazz albums
4. Key member of Miles Davis's quintet and sextet
5. Pioneered modal jazz and free jazz approaches on saxophone
6. His career spanned hard bop, modal jazz, and avant-garde/free jazz

## Context & Application

Coltrane's career traced a remarkable arc from bebop sideman to hard bop virtuoso to modal innovator to free jazz pioneer. His time with Miles Davis's groups in the mid-to-late 1950s was formative for both musicians, and his subsequent solo career pushed jazz toward ever-greater harmonic and rhythmic freedom ([john-coltrane](/source/john-coltrane/)).
