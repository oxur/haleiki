## Quick Definition

Arbuscular mycorrhiza (AM) is the most common type of mycorrhizal association, in which fungi of the phylum Glomeromycota penetrate plant root cells and form branching, tree-like structures called arbuscules for nutrient exchange. It is present in approximately 70-85% of plant species.

## Core Definition

Arbuscular mycorrhizas have hyphae that penetrate plant cells, producing branching, tree-like structures called arbuscules within the plant cells for nutrient exchange. The structure of the arbuscules greatly increases the contact surface area between the hypha and the host cell cytoplasm to facilitate nutrient transfer ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)). Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi are obligate biotrophs, entirely dependent upon their plant hosts for growth and reproduction, having lost the ability to decompose dead plant material. Twenty percent of the photosynthetic products of the host plant are consumed by the fungi, exchanged for equal amounts of phosphate ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)).

## Key Properties

1. Most common mycorrhizal type: present in 70-85% of plant species
2. Formed only by fungi of the phylum Glomeromycota
3. Obligate biotrophs: cannot survive without plant hosts
4. Intracellular penetration: arbuscules form inside root cells for nutrient exchange
5. Plant provides 20% of photosynthetic products; fungus provides phosphate
6. Evolved 400-460 million years ago, the oldest mycorrhizal type
7. Estimated 244-1,600 species of AM fungi
8. Generally host-nonspecific (generalists)
9. Produce glomalin, a glycoprotein that may be a major soil carbon store

## Context & Application

AM fungi are crucial for global agriculture since most crop species depend on them. Fossil evidence from the Rhynie chert (407 million years ago) shows arbuscular-like structures in early land plants, suggesting AM fungi were key to plant terrestrialization ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)): Wheat has arbuscular mycorrhiza, with AM fungi providing phosphorus in exchange for photosynthetic carbon.

## Relationships

- **Ectomycorrhiza** is the contrasting major type, with intercellular rather than intracellular fungal growth
- **Mycorrhiza** is the broader concept of which AM is the most widespread subtype
- **Fungi** of the phylum Glomeromycota exclusively form AM associations
