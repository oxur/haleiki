## Quick Definition

The mind-body problem is the central question in philosophy of mind concerning the relationship between mental processes and physical bodily states. It asks how (or whether) subjective conscious experience can arise from physical brain processes.

## Core Definition

The mind-body problem concerns the explanation of the relationship that exists between minds, or mental processes, and bodily states or processes. The main aim of philosophers working in this area is to determine the nature of the mind and mental states/processes, and how minds are affected by and can affect the body ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Dualism and monism are the two central schools of thought on the mind-body problem. Descartes was the first to clearly identify the mind with consciousness and self-awareness, and to distinguish this from the brain, formulating the problem in the form in which it still exists today ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)).

## Key Properties

1. Paradigmatic issue in philosophy of mind since Descartes (17th century)
2. Two central schools: dualism (mind and body are distinct) and monism (mind and body are one)
3. Encompasses questions about consciousness, mental causation, and subjective experience
4. Connected to problems of qualia, intentionality, and the hard problem of consciousness
5. Has practical implications for cognitive science, AI, and neuroscience

## Context & Application

The mind-body problem has influenced multiple scientific fields. Physicalist approaches have been particularly influential in sociobiology, computer science (especially AI), evolutionary psychology, and the neurosciences ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)). Modern philosophers continue to ask how the subjective qualities and intentionality of mental states can be explained in naturalistic terms. Some philosophers (Wittgensteinians) argue the problem is illusory, arising from conceptual confusion.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): A child touches a hot stove (physical event) which causes pain (mental event) and makes her yell (physical event), illustrating the puzzle of how physical and mental events interact.

**Example 2** ([philosophy-of-mind](/source/philosophy-of-mind/)): The "zombie argument" by David Chalmers asks whether a being physically identical to a human but lacking consciousness is conceivable.

## Relationships

- **Dualism** is one major approach, arguing mind and body are distinct
- **Physicalism** is the dominant monist approach, arguing mental states are physical
- **Qualia** are the subjective qualities of experience that make the problem difficult
- **Consciousness** is the central phenomenon the problem attempts to explain
- **Functionalism** is a physicalist approach defining mental states by their causal roles
