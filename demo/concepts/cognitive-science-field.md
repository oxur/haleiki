## Quick Definition

Cognitive science is the interdisciplinary scientific study of the mind and its processes, drawing from psychology, philosophy, artificial intelligence, neuroscience, linguistics, and anthropology. Its central tenet is that thinking can be understood in terms of representational structures and computational procedures.

## Core Definition

Cognitive science is the interdisciplinary, scientific study of the mind and its processes. It examines the nature, tasks, and functions of cognition. Mental faculties of concern include perception, memory, attention, reasoning, language, and emotion ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)). One of its fundamental concepts is that "thinking can best be understood in terms of representational structures in the mind and computational procedures that operate on those structures." The term was coined by Christopher Longuet-Higgins in 1973 ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)).

## Key Properties

1. Interdisciplinary: draws from psychology, philosophy, AI, neuroscience, linguistics, and anthropology
2. Emerged from the cognitive revolution of the 1950s
3. Central tenet: thinking involves representational structures and computational procedures
4. Multi-level analysis: from neural circuits to modular brain organization
5. Uses the scientific method, simulation, and computational modeling
6. Term coined in 1973; first department founded at UCSD in 1986

## Context & Application

Cognitive science has been shaped by the cognitive revolution's rejection of behaviorism, the development of computational models of mind (symbolic AI and connectionism), and the rise of embodied cognition approaches that emphasize the role of body and environment. David Marr's three levels of analysis (computational theory, representation/algorithms, hardware implementation) provide a key analytical framework ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)): Noam Chomsky's 1959 review of Skinner's Verbal Behavior argued that language requires a theory of generative grammar with internal representations, challenging the behaviorist paradigm.

**Example 2** ([cognitive-science](/source/cognitive-science/)): The dichotic listening task demonstrates selective attention: subjects told to focus on one ear cannot report the content of the unattended message.

## Relationships

- **Mind-body problem** provides philosophical foundations for cognitive science
- **Consciousness** is a core topic, though classical cognitivists initially de-emphasized it
- **Perception** is a central mental faculty studied by cognitive scientists
- **Functionalism** provides a philosophical framework adopted by many cognitive scientists
