## Quick Definition

A musical temperament is a tuning system that determines the precise size of intervals between notes. Equal temperament, the most common system today, divides the octave into twelve equal semitones.

## Core Definition

Musical tuning systems, or temperaments, determine the precise size of intervals. Tuning systems vary widely within and between world cultures. In Western culture, there have long been several competing tuning systems, all with different qualities. Internationally, the system known as equal temperament is most commonly used today because it is considered the most satisfactory compromise that allows instruments of fixed tuning (e.g. the piano) to sound acceptably in tune in all keys ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Key Properties

1. Determines the exact size of intervals between pitches
2. Varies across cultures and historical periods
3. Equal temperament divides the octave into 12 equal semitones
4. Just intonation uses pure mathematical ratios for intervals
5. Pythagorean tuning is based on perfect fifths
6. Each temperament involves trade-offs between purity of different intervals

## Context & Application

The adoption of equal temperament was a pivotal development in Western music, enabling instruments to play in all keys equally well. Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier demonstrated the musical possibilities of a well-tempered (though not necessarily equal-tempered) keyboard tuning. The Baroque-era doctrine of the affections associated specific emotional qualities with specific keys, but equal temperament largely erased these distinctions ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).
