## Quick Definition

Symbiosis is a close, long-term biological interaction between two or more different species. It encompasses mutualistic (mutually beneficial), parasitic (one benefits at the other's expense), and commensal (one benefits, other unaffected) relationships.

## Core Definition

Lichens are the lifeform that first brought the term symbiosis (as Symbiotismus) into biological context, representing a mutualistic association between fungi and algae or cyanobacteria ([lichen](/source/lichen/)). Mycorrhiza is a symbiotic association between a fungus and a plant; the association is normally mutualistic, though in particular species or circumstances it may be parasitic ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)). The term encompasses a range of interspecific relationships from obligate mutualism to parasitism.

## Key Properties

1. Close, long-term biological interaction between different species
2. Types include mutualism, parasitism, and commensalism
3. Can be obligate (species cannot survive alone) or facultative
4. Lichens introduced the term to biology
5. Mycorrhizal symbiosis is the most widespread example in terrestrial ecosystems

## Context & Application

Symbiosis is one of the most important ecological relationships on Earth. Over 90% of land plants depend on mycorrhizal symbiosis for nutrient uptake. Lichens colonize environments inaccessible to either partner alone. The concept has expanded from its original narrow meaning (mutualism) to encompass all close interspecific associations.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([lichen](/source/lichen/)): Lichens are hybrid colonies of algae or cyanobacteria living symbiotically among fungal filaments, representing the classic example that introduced the term.

**Example 2** ([mycorrhiza](/source/mycorrhiza/)): Mycorrhizal fungi form symbiotic associations with ~95% of investigated plant families, exchanging mineral nutrients for photosynthetic carbon.

## Relationships

- **Mycorrhiza** is a fungal-plant symbiosis
- **Lichen** is a fungal-algal/cyanobacterial symbiosis
