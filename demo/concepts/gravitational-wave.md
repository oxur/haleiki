## Quick Definition

Gravitational waves are ripples in the metric of spacetime that propagate at the speed of light, predicted by Einstein in 1916 and directly detected by LIGO in 2016. They are produced by accelerating masses, most dramatically by merging black holes or neutron stars.

## Core Definition

Gravitational waves are ripples in the metric of spacetime that propagate at the speed of light, analogous to electromagnetic waves. They are produced by accelerating masses and carry energy away from their sources. On February 11, 2016, the Advanced LIGO team announced the first direct detection of gravitational waves from a pair of merging black holes ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Prerequisites

- **General relativity** -- gravitational waves are solutions of the linearized Einstein field equations.
- **Einstein field equations** -- the wave solutions arise from perturbation analysis of the field equations.

## Key Properties

1. **Propagate at light speed** -- travel at the speed of light through spacetime ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
2. **Transverse** -- deform space perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
3. **Extremely weak** -- typical relative distance changes are of order $10^{-21}$ or less ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
4. **Carry energy** -- binary systems lose orbital energy through gravitational wave emission, causing orbital decay ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).
5. **Nonlinear effects** -- arbitrarily strong gravitational waves do not obey linear superposition ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Context & Application

Predicted by Einstein in 1916, gravitational waves were first indirectly detected through the orbital decay of the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar PSR 1913+16 (Nobel Prize 1993). LIGO's 2016 direct detection opened the field of gravitational-wave astronomy. Space-based detectors like LISA aim to detect lower-frequency waves from supermassive black hole mergers ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): A ring of freely floating test particles is rhythmically deformed by a passing gravitational wave -- stretched in one direction and compressed in the perpendicular direction.

**Example 2** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): The binary pulsar PSR J0737-3039 shows orbital decay in precise agreement with general relativity's prediction of energy loss through gravitational wave emission.

## Relationships

- **Related to spacetime**: gravitational waves are propagating disturbances in spacetime geometry.
- **Related to black-hole**: merging black holes are the strongest sources of gravitational waves.
