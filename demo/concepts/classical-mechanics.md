## Quick Definition

Classical mechanics is the branch of physics concerned with the motion of bodies acted on by forces, described by Newton's laws of motion. It is the foundation of classical physics and provides an accurate description of macroscopic, low-speed phenomena.

## Core Definition

Classical mechanics is concerned with bodies acted on by forces and bodies in motion. It may be divided into statics (study of forces on bodies not subject to acceleration), kinematics (study of motion without regard to its causes), and dynamics (study of motion and the forces that affect it). Mechanics may also be divided into solid mechanics and fluid mechanics. Classical mechanics predicted a wide variety of observations and became the basis for classical physics, though it breaks down at atomic scales (requiring quantum mechanics) and at high speeds (requiring special relativity) ([physics](/source/physics/)).

At the base of classical mechanics is the notion that a body's motion can be described as a combination of free (inertial) motion and deviations caused by external forces, in accordance with Newton's second law of motion ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Three divisions** -- statics, kinematics, and dynamics ([physics](/source/physics/)).
2. **Deterministic** -- given initial conditions, the trajectory of a system is fully determined.
3. **Continuous** -- approximates nature as continuous, unlike quantum mechanics which introduces discreteness ([physics](/source/physics/)).
4. **Limiting case** -- derivable as a limiting case of both quantum mechanics (large scales) and special relativity (low speeds) ([physics](/source/physics/)).

## Context & Application

Classical mechanics was formalized by Isaac Newton in the 17th century. Newton's laws of motion and law of universal gravitation unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics. Lagrange and Hamilton later reformulated mechanics in terms of energy principles, extending its mathematical power. Classical mechanics remains the workhorse of engineering, from bridge design to spacecraft trajectories ([physics](/source/physics/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([physics](/source/physics/)): Galileo's law of falling bodies describes the parabolic trajectory of objects under gravity, a foundational result of classical mechanics.

**Example 2** ([general-relativity](/source/general-relativity/)): The preferred inertial motions of classical mechanics -- objects moving along straight lines at constant speed -- are the starting point for understanding how general relativity generalizes this to curved spacetime geodesics.
