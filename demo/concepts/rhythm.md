## Quick Definition

Rhythm is the sequential arrangement of sounds and silences in time. It encompasses meter, beat, tempo, and the patterns of duration that give music its temporal structure.

## Core Definition

Rhythm is produced by the sequential arrangement of sounds and silences in time. Meter measures music in regular pulse groupings, called measures or bars. The time signature or meter signature specifies how many beats are in a measure, and which value of written note is counted or felt as a single beat ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)). Through increased stress, or variations in duration or articulation, particular tones may be accented. Syncopated rhythms contradict conventional accentuation by accenting unexpected parts of the beat. Playing simultaneous rhythms in more than one time signature is called polyrhythm ([music-theory](/source/music-theory/)).

## Key Properties

1. Rhythm is the temporal organization of sounds and silences
2. Meter provides regular pulse groupings measured in bars
3. Time signatures specify beats per measure and beat value
4. Accents reinforce or contradict the meter through stress, duration, or articulation
5. Syncopation accents unexpected parts of the beat
6. Polyrhythm involves simultaneous use of multiple time signatures

## Context & Application

Rhythm is fundamental to all musical traditions. In medieval music, the development of rhythmic notation was a major achievement, beginning with rhythmic modes in the 13th century and evolving through mensural notation ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)). Jazz is particularly characterized by its distinctive rhythmic features, including swing feel, syncopation, and polyrhythms drawn from African musical traditions ([jazz](/source/jazz/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([medieval-music](/source/medieval-music/)): The rhythmic modes codified by Johannes de Garlandia around 1250 were the first written rhythmic system in Western music, establishing six species of rhythmic patterns based on combinations of longs and breves.

**Example 2** ([jazz](/source/jazz/)): Jazz is characterized by swing and polyrhythms, combining African rhythmic traditions with European harmonic structures.

## Relationships

- **melody** -- Rhythm is one of the basic elements of melody, providing its temporal framework
- **harmony** -- Rhythmic patterns interact with harmonic progressions to create musical texture
- **syncopation** -- Syncopation is a specific rhythmic technique that contradicts expected accentuation
- **polyrhythm** -- Polyrhythm is the simultaneous use of conflicting rhythmic patterns
