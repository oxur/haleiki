## Quick Definition

Artificial consciousness is the hypothesized possibility that artificial intelligence systems could achieve consciousness or subjective experience. The field draws from philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and neuroscience to ask whether and how consciousness could be synthesized in engineered artifacts.

## Core Definition

Artificial consciousness, also known as machine consciousness or synthetic consciousness, is consciousness hypothesized to be possible for artificial intelligence. It is also the corresponding field of study, which draws insights from philosophy of mind, philosophy of artificial intelligence, cognitive science, and neuroscience ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)). Type-identity theorists argue consciousness requires particular physical substrates, while functionalists hold that any system instantiating the right causal pattern could be conscious regardless of physical constitution ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Consciousness** — Understanding what consciousness is must precede asking whether machines can have it
- **Functionalism** — The philosophical position most supportive of artificial consciousness possibility

## Key Properties

1. Central debate: whether consciousness is substrate-independent (functionalism) or requires biological substrates (type-identity theory)
2. Chalmers' thought experiments ("fading qualia," "dancing qualia") argue for functional isomorphism implying identical consciousness
3. Testing for machine consciousness faces fundamental challenges: sentience is inherently first-person
4. The Turing test assesses behavioral mimicry, not sentience
5. Ethical implications: if AI achieves sentience, it may warrant welfare concerns and legal protection
6. LLMs like LaMDA display humanlike responses but scientific consensus holds they likely lack consciousness

## Context & Application

David Chalmers argued in 2023 that current LLMs likely lack consciousness because they miss features like recurrent processing, a global workspace, and unified agency, but that future extended models might meet criteria for consciousness ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)). Thomas Metzinger argued for a moratorium on synthetic phenomenology until 2050 due to the risk of creating "an explosion of artificial suffering." Critics like Anil Seth argue that the complexity of biological neurons intrinsically matters beyond their function.

## Examples

**Example 1** ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)): Chalmers' "fading qualia" thought experiment argues that gradually replacing brain neurons with functionally identical silicon chips would not cause consciousness to fade, supporting substrate independence.

**Example 2** ([artificial-consciousness](/source/artificial-consciousness/)): In 2022, Google engineer Blake Lemoine claimed LaMDA was sentient; the scientific community judged this as likely mimicry rather than machine sentience.

## Relationships

- **Hard problem of consciousness** applies to artificial systems: even functional duplication may not produce subjective experience
- **Qualia** are what artificial consciousness would need to possess to be truly conscious
- **Cognitive science** provides empirical frameworks for studying consciousness that inform AI approaches
- **Functionalism** is the philosophical position most supportive of artificial consciousness
