## Quick Definition

Vimalamitra was an 8th-century Indian Buddhist monk and Dzogchen master whose teachers included Buddhaguhya, Jnanasutra, and Sri Singha. He transmitted the Vima Nyingtik teachings to Tibet and is one of the founding figures of the Nyingma Dzogchen lineage.

## Core Definition

Vimalamitra was an 8th-century Indian Buddhist monk whose teachers were Buddhaguhya, Jnanasutra, and Sri Singha ([vimalamitra](/source/vimalamitra/)). He was one of the eight teachers of the great Indian adept Padmasambhava ([vimalamitra](/source/vimalamitra/)). The earliest Dzogchen texts, the Eighteen Great Scriptures of the Mind Series, are attributed to Indian masters including Vimalamitra ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)). The instructional Nyingtik teachings include the Vima Nyingtik, the detailed teachings brought to Tibet by Vimalamitra, mainly based on the Seventeen Tantras ([longchen-nyingtik](/source/longchen-nyingtik/)). He was supposed to have vowed to take rebirth every hundred years ([vimalamitra](/source/vimalamitra/)).

## Key Properties

1. **Vima Nyingtik** -- brought the detailed Nyingtik teachings to Tibet, the scholarly tradition based on the Seventeen Tantras ([longchen-nyingtik](/source/longchen-nyingtik/)).
2. **Teacher of Padmasambhava** -- one of the eight teachers of Padmasambhava ([vimalamitra](/source/vimalamitra/)).
3. **Rebirth vow** -- said to have vowed to take rebirth every hundred years, with notable figures identified as incarnations ([vimalamitra](/source/vimalamitra/)).
4. **Rainbow body** -- some exceptional practitioners including Vimalamitra are held to have realized a rainbow body without dying ([dzogchen](/source/dzogchen/)).

## Context & Application

The eighteen major works of the Inner Tantra were transmitted and translated by Padmasambhava and Vimalamitra ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). The Kama lineage begins with Padmasambhava, Shantarakshita, Vimalamitra, and Vairochana ([nyingma](/source/nyingma/)). Longchenpa later became the lineage holder of both the Vima Nyingtik and the Khandro Nyingtik, unifying these two major traditions ([longchen-nyingtik](/source/longchen-nyingtik/)).
