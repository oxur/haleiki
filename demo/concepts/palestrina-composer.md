## Quick Definition

Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina (c. 1525-1594) was an Italian composer of late Renaissance music, considered the leading composer of his era. His style of counterpoint became the model for sacred polyphonic composition.

## Core Definition

Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina was an Italian composer of late Renaissance music. The central representative of the Roman School, Palestrina is considered the leading composer of late 16th-century Europe ([palestrina](/source/palestrina/)). Primarily known for his masses and motets (numbering over 105 and 250 respectively), Palestrina had a long-lasting influence on the development of church and secular music in Europe, especially on the development of counterpoint. It was the "Palestrinian style" of counterpoint -- especially as codified by Johann Joseph Fux -- rather than his individual compositions that exerted the greatest influence ([palestrina](/source/palestrina/)).

## Key Properties

1. Central representative of the Roman School of composition
2. Composed over 105 masses, 250+ motets, 140+ madrigals
3. His counterpoint style became the pedagogical model codified by J.J. Fux
4. Successfully reconciled aesthetic and functional aims of Catholic church music during the Counter-Reformation
5. One of the few Renaissance composers never entirely forgotten
6. Served as maestro di cappella at the Cappella Giulia, St. Peter's Basilica

## Context & Application

Palestrina's music represents the culmination of Renaissance polyphonic style. His ability to combine the functional needs of the Catholic Church with the prevailing musical styles during the Counter-Reformation period earned him enduring fame as the ideal Catholic composer ([palestrina](/source/palestrina/)). The florid counterpoint of Palestrina and the Roman School represents the pinnacle of late Renaissance music ([renaissance-music](/source/renaissance-music/)).
