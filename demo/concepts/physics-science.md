## Quick Definition

Physics is the scientific study of matter, its fundamental constituents, its motion and behavior through space and time, and the related entities of energy and force. It is one of the most fundamental scientific disciplines.

## Core Definition

Physics is the scientific study of matter, its fundamental constituents, its motion and behavior through space and time, and the related entities of energy and force ([physics](/source/physics/)). Physics is one of the oldest academic disciplines and is considered "the fundamental science" because all branches of natural science, including chemistry, astronomy, geology, and biology, are constrained by the laws of physics ([physics](/source/physics/)). The field is broadly divided into classical physics and modern physics, with the latter encompassing quantum mechanics and relativity.

## Key Properties

1. **Fundamental science** -- physics underpins all other natural sciences; its laws constrain chemistry, biology, geology, and astronomy ([physics](/source/physics/)).
2. **Two-pillar structure** -- modern physics rests on two chief theories: quantum mechanics (governing the very small) and general relativity (governing gravity and cosmology) ([physics](/source/physics/)).
3. **Theory-experiment interplay** -- progress comes from the dynamic interaction between theoretical predictions and experimental verification ([physics](/source/physics/)).
4. **Mathematical language** -- physics uses mathematics to organize experimental results and formulate precise, testable predictions ([physics](/source/physics/)).
5. **Technological driver** -- advances in physics have led directly to transformative technologies, from electromagnetism enabling computers to nuclear physics enabling both power and weapons ([physics](/source/physics/)).

## Context & Application

Physics has roots in ancient astronomy and Greek natural philosophy. The Scientific Revolution of the 17th century, with Galileo, Kepler, and Newton, established physics as a separate quantitative science. The 19th century saw the unification of electricity and magnetism and the development of thermodynamics. The 20th century brought the twin revolutions of quantum mechanics and relativity, transforming the conceptual basis of the discipline ([physics](/source/physics/)).
