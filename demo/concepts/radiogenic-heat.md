## Quick Definition

Radiogenic heat is the thermal energy produced by the radioactive decay of isotopes in Earth's mantle and crust. It accounts for approximately 50% of Earth's total internal heat output.

## Core Definition

About 50% of Earth's internal heat originates from radioactive decay. Four radioactive isotopes are responsible for the majority of radiogenic heat: uranium-238, uranium-235, thorium-232, and potassium-40 ([earths-internal-heat-budget](/source/earths-internal-heat-budget/)). Chemical and physical models give estimated ranges of 15-41 TW for radiogenic heat. The radioactive decay of elements in Earth's mantle and crust results in production of daughter isotopes and release of geoneutrinos and heat energy ([earths-internal-heat-budget](/source/earths-internal-heat-budget/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Mantle** — Radiogenic heat is produced primarily in the mantle and crust
- **Mantle convection** — Radiogenic heat is one of the drivers of mantle convection

## Key Properties

1. Accounts for approximately 50% of Earth's total internal heat (~15-41 TW)
2. Four primary isotopes: uranium-238, uranium-235, thorium-232, and potassium-40
3. Earth's core is unlikely to be a significant source due to low radioactive element concentrations
4. Can be measured indirectly through geoneutrino detection (for U-238 and Th-232)
5. Was much greater in early Earth due to shorter half-life isotopes (U-235, K-40)

## Context & Application

Radiogenic heat, combined with primordial heat, drives all of Earth's geological processes including mantle convection, plate tectonics, mountain building, rock metamorphism, and volcanism. Despite its geological significance, Earth's interior heat contributes only 0.03% of Earth's total energy budget at the surface, which is dominated by solar radiation ([earths-internal-heat-budget](/source/earths-internal-heat-budget/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([earths-internal-heat-budget](/source/earths-internal-heat-budget/)): Thorium-232, with a half-life of 14 billion years and a mean mantle concentration of 124 x 10^-9 kg/kg, releases 26.4 x 10^-6 W per kg of isotope.

## Relationships

- **Primordial heat** is the other major internal heat source, contrasting as leftover heat from formation rather than actively produced
- **Mantle convection** is driven in part by radiogenic heat
