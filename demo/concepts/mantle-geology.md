## Quick Definition

A mantle is a layer inside a planetary body bounded below by a core and above by a crust, typically the largest and most massive layer. Earth's mantle is a 2,900-km-thick layer of silicate rock that, though predominantly solid, flows viscously over geological timescales.

## Core Definition

A mantle is a layer inside a planetary body bounded below by a core and above by a crust, made of rock or ices, and generally the largest and most massive layer of the planetary body ([mantle](/source/mantle/)). Earth's mantle is a layer of silicate rock between the crust and the outer core, with a mass of 4.01 x 10^24 kg (67% of Earth's mass) and a thickness of 2,900 km, making up about 84% of Earth's volume ([mantle](/source/mantle/)). It is divided into upper and lower mantle separated by a transition zone, with the lowest part next to the core-mantle boundary known as the D" layer ([internal-structure-of-earth](/source/internal-structure-of-earth/)).

## Prerequisites

- **Lithosphere** — Understanding the rigid outer layer helps contextualize the mantle as the layer beneath

## Key Properties

1. Thickness of 2,900 km, comprising about 84% of Earth's volume
2. Composed of silicate rocks richer in iron and magnesium than the overlying crust
3. Predominantly solid but flows viscously over geological timescales
4. Viscosity ranges between 10^21 and 10^24 pascal-seconds, increasing with depth
5. Divided into upper mantle, transition zone, and lower mantle
6. Partial melting at mid-ocean ridges produces oceanic crust; partial melting at subduction zones produces continental crust
7. Present in all terrestrial planets and some moons and asteroids

## Context & Application

The mantle is central to plate tectonics: convection of the mantle propels the motion of tectonic plates in the crust ([internal-structure-of-earth](/source/internal-structure-of-earth/)). The source of heat that drives this motion is the decay of radioactive isotopes combined with primordial heat from planetary formation. Earth's mantle also plays a critical role in the planet's geochemistry, as it serves as the source material for both oceanic and continental crust through partial melting processes ([mantle](/source/mantle/)).

## Examples

**Example 1** ([mantle](/source/mantle/)): Earth's mantle has a mass of 4.01 x 10^24 kg, making up 67% of the total mass, with a thickness of 2,900 km.

**Example 2** ([mantle](/source/mantle/)): Mercury has a silicate mantle approximately 490 km thick (28% of its mass), Venus has one approximately 2,800 km thick (70% of its mass), and Mars has one approximately 1,600 km thick.

## Relationships

- **Mantle convection** is the process by which heat drives circulation within the mantle
- **Earth's crust** sits above the mantle, separated by the Mohorovicic discontinuity
- **Asthenosphere** is the mechanically weak upper portion of the mantle beneath the lithosphere
- **Plate tectonics** is driven by convection within the mantle

## Common Confusions

- **Mantle is not liquid**: Though it flows on geological timescales, the mantle is predominantly solid rock
- **Mantle vs. asthenosphere**: The asthenosphere is only the upper, mechanically weaker portion of the mantle; the mantle extends much deeper
