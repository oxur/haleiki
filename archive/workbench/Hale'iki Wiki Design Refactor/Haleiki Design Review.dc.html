Haleʻiki · Design Unification Review

One house, two dialects

The fifteen mockups are beautiful — but they drifted into two overlapping design systems: the wiki set (home, concept, category, browse, attribution) and the news + search set. This board flags where they disagree, and shows the unified resolution rendered live from the new single stylesheet.

Two footers — and they disagree on the content license

The wiki footer says content is CC BY-SA 4.0; the news/search footer says CC0. It also swaps the tagline and the whole link set. This is a factual contradiction a reader will notice — it needs a single source of truth (the site.profile context).

B

Two top navs — dead search box vs. live one, and three different “active” conventions

The wiki pages ship an empty search slot (a commented-out placeholder) and only Browse/About links. The news/search pages have a real search field, a fuller link set, and an active state — but spelled three ways (.active, .active-link, and a bare current link). Unify to one bar with a working search and aria-current.

C

Same class name, two different components

Both the home page and the news “Posts by Category” page define .category-card / .category-grid — with different inner markup. In one stylesheet they'd overwrite each other. Fix: the home component keeps the canonical name; the news variant is namespaced .news-cat-card. Both shown below, now coexisting.

The home hero search is disabled — it reads as broken

E

A question for you: should the trust bar sit on news posts?

You asked to wire the trust bar into concept, source and news posts. It's built and ready for all three. But epistemic trust describes the sourcing of knowledge — a launch announcement isn't “credible vs. speculative” the way a concept card is. My recommendation: trust bar on concept + source only, and give news posts a plain author/date meta line instead. Both are wired — your call. Trust bar shown here (credible = sage):

Mark-and-Sweep

Memory ManagementGarbage CollectionIntermediate
Quick Definition

A garbage-collection algorithm that reclaims memory in two phases: marking all reachable objects from the roots, then sweeping the heap to free the unmarked ones.

Contrasts With

Reference Counting — incremental, but cannot reclaim cycles without help; mark-and-sweep handles cycles naturally.
